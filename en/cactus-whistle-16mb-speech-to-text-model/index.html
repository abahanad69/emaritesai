Cactus releases Whistle, a 16.9MB speech-to-text model for CPUs

The open model transcribes seven languages on ordinary processors, from phones and watches to browsers.

2026-10-04 · EmaritesAI

Cactus Compute released Whistle on October 2, a speech-to-text model that fits in a single 16.9MB file and runs on a regular processor with no graphics card. The weights are on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license.

Whistle transcribes seven languages: English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Polish. Arabic is not among them. Cactus says the engine runs on 17 targets, including macOS, Windows on ARM, Linux, Android, iOS, watchOS, RISC-V chips and web browsers.

The company compares Whistle with OpenAI's Whisper base, a widely used open model that is about 145.3MB. According to Cactus, Whistle has lower error rates on LibriSpeech, SPGISpeech, Earnings-22 and the FLEURS multilingual average. On an Apple M4 Pro processor with 10 seconds of audio, it produced its first token in 11.1 milliseconds, against 73.2 for Whisper base, and decoded 1,319 tokens per second compared with 266.

These are the company's own measurements. Small speech models matter because they let apps transcribe voice notes, meetings or commands directly on a device, which keeps audio private and works without an internet connection.

Whistle sits alongside Needle, Cactus's family of tiny models for tool calls and text extraction on phones, wearables and other small devices. Developers have already started requests to add it to open-source dictation apps.
