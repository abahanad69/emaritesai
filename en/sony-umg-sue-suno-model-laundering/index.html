Sony and UMG sue Suno again, alleging 'model laundering'

The labels say Suno's v6 inherits infringement by training on outputs of earlier models.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Sony Music and Universal Music Group have gone after Suno again, The Verge reports, this time targeting the company's newest model.

In a complaint shared with The Verge, the labels claim Suno's v6 still infringes their copyrights because it was trained on user outputs from previous Suno models — which, they allege, were themselves trained on music taken without a licence from YouTube and other sources. They call this "model laundering": training a new model on the outputs of an infringing one, they argue, does not remove the infringement but passes it along. "V6 is not a fresh start," the complaint says.

Suno has described v6 as trained from the ground up on a new set of data. A company spokesperson told The Verge that v6 was trained on content licensed from Suno's partners, on user creations and preference signals from its community, and on its team's accumulated learnings.

Sony and UMG are notable holdouts: they did not sign a licensing agreement with Suno, while other rights holders have struck deals with AI music companies.

The case could test a question that reaches well beyond music — whether a model trained on synthetic data can carry legal liability for how the earlier model that produced that data was built.
