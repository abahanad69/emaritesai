GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 crack unsolved Enigma messages

Two cryptanalysts used OpenAI and Anthropic models to break WWII messages that had resisted experts for years.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Two unsolved messages encrypted on German Enigma machines during the Second World War have been broken with the help of AI models from OpenAI and Anthropic, TechCrunch reports.

Developer Carter Leffen asked OpenAI's newest model, GPT-6 Astra, to search a database of Enigma traffic for an unbroken message and decode it. According to the report, the model researched archives, gathered context clues, built its own Enigma simulator and recovered the plaintext of a message that had resisted researchers since 2005. Frode Weierud, a retired engineer who maintains the Crypto Cellar archive of Enigma messages, validated the solution last week.

Weierud said the model worked like a professional cryptanalyst and archive researcher, achieving in two days what could take a human weeks or months. He also noted that Astra's logs refer to messages in a "private collection" he does not host, and he is unsure whether the model actually accessed them.

On September 21, cybersecurity executive Jack Willis told Weierud he had broken a different message using Anthropic's Claude Opus 5. Willis gave the model considerably more guidance, and it used the known signature of a particular officer to crack the text.

Weierud says seven Enigma messages now remain unbroken, plus one whose plaintext is known but whose encryption has not been broken.

3 things to know
  1. GPT-6 Astra decoded an Enigma message unsolved since 2005
  2. Claude Opus 5 broke a second message with more human guidance
  3. Only seven Enigma messages are still unbroken
