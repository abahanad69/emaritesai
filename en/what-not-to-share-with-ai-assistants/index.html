What not to share with an AI assistant, at work or at home

Five habits that keep your passwords, clients and home address out of the wrong hands.

2026-09-30 · EmaritesAI

AI assistants are now handling our emails, documents and even Marketplace sales. This week a YouTuber said Meta's Muse agent shared his home address with a buyer after he gave it permanent permission. A few habits keep that kind of mistake from happening to you.

Never paste secrets into a chat: passwords, API keys, card or bank details, or ID numbers. No assistant needs them to help you, and chats can be stored or reviewed.

At work, strip identifying details before asking for help. Replace client names, contract numbers and personal data with placeholders; the AI can still fix the wording or the formula.

Use the tools your employer has approved, and check the settings: many services let you turn off using your chats for training, and business plans usually have stricter data terms.

With agents, choose one-time permissions until you trust how they behave. "Allow always" can mean the agent stops asking before it acts.

Finally, keep approvals on for anything that shares an address, spends money, sends files or messages people outside your circle. A few seconds of checking beats undoing a leak.

Step by step
  1. Never paste secrets: Passwords, API keys, card and bank details don't belong in any chat.
  2. Strip client data: Swap names, IDs and contract numbers for placeholders before asking.
  3. Use approved tools: Stick to work-approved AI and turn off chat training where you can.
  4. Allow once, not always: Give agents one-time permissions until you trust them.
  5. Keep approvals on: Check before an agent shares an address, spends money or sends files.
3 things to know
  1. Never paste passwords, keys or bank details into a chat
  2. Strip client names and IDs before asking for help
  3. Give agents one-time permissions, not 'Allow always'
