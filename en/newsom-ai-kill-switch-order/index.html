Newsom orders California to weigh an AI 'kill switch' mandate

An executive order gives an expert group two months to recommend tougher AI safety rules for state law.

2026-09-19 · EmaritesAI

California is moving to set the pace on AI oversight while Congress stalls. Governor Gavin Newsom issued an executive order on Friday directing the state to convene a group of experts and deliver recommendations within two months on strengthening AI safety measures in state law, The Verge reported.

The order asks the group to weigh several specific mechanisms: requiring AI companies to host independent verification teams onsite for regular audits; holding transparency reports and risk assessments to independent auditing standards; creating a "kill switch" that is routinely verified as effective; and requiring companies to report loss-of-control incidents as critical safety incidents.

It also tells a state agency to accelerate two laws Newsom already signed, which set up a framework for independent verifiers and a state registry of AI auditors.

The timing matters. Washington is unlikely to move before the midterms, with the House in recess until November, so state rules are where the near-term constraints on frontier labs are most likely to appear. California hosts most of the largest AI developers, which gives a state mandate outsized reach.

The order sets a process, not a rule: nothing becomes law until the recommendations land and the legislature acts.

3 things to know
  1. Executive order gives experts two months to recommend AI safety rules
  2. Ideas include a verified kill switch and onsite independent auditors
  3. Loss-of-control incidents would be reported as critical safety incidents
