Finland orders Google to halt work at two data centre sites

Regulators say forest clearing at Muhos went ahead without a required environmental impact assessment.

2026-10-07 · EmaritesAI

Finnish authorities have told Google to stop building work at two planned data centre sites over the clearing of more than 300 hectares of forest, the BBC reports. The sites are part of a €13 billion ($15 billion) investment in Finland that Google announced less than a month ago, which it calls its largest single investment in Europe.

The Finnish Supervisory Agency (LVV) said work at one site went ahead without a mandatory environmental impact assessment. It ordered measures that would significantly alter the environment to be suspended by October 23. The orders cover preparatory work at Muhos and Kajaani, including tree and topsoil removal, site roads, storage areas and changes to ditches.

Tuike Finland, the company running Google's data centre projects, must explain its plans by October 14, and the agency says it may start enforcement proceedings if the company does not comply.

Google told the BBC it had fallen short of its own standards, but said it acted in good faith under Finland's Forestry Act and carried out nature surveys. It plans tree planting across 130 hectares at Muhos.

Google says the Finnish sites will serve demand for Search, Maps and Gemini. Finland's cool climate and low-carbon electricity have made it a target for data centre builders, and the case shows how fast AI construction is colliding with local environmental rules.
