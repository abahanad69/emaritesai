DeepMind and Singapore build an on-phone AI that flags scam chats

A small Gemma model trained on local scam patterns checks conversations on the device and warns users before they send money or data.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

Google DeepMind and the Government Technology Agency of Singapore (GovTech) have built a scam detection model that runs entirely on a smartphone. They presented it on October 14 at Singapore International Cyber Week.

The problem is large. According to the two partners, scams make up more than half of Singapore's reported crimes and have cost residents over S$4 billion since 2019. Up to 80% of those losses come from conversational scams, where a fraudster builds trust over chat before asking for money or details.

Many messaging apps use end-to-end encryption, so a server cannot read the messages. The new system works around that by staying on the phone. A small, low-power classifier screens incoming messages first. If one looks suspicious, a fine-tuned Gemma model reads a longer stretch of the conversation and either confirms a scam alert or clears it. When it confirms a threat, the user sees a warning to pause before sending money or personal information.

The model was trained on local scam patterns, including fake bank staff and impersonated government agencies, inside GovTech's isolated cloud environment. DeepMind says training used anonymised scam reports and intelligence feeds, with no personal data from citizens. "Fighting scams shouldn't mean sacrificing personal privacy," said Mark Chen, GovTech's director of trust and safety.

The partners have not published accuracy figures or a launch date. They suggest messaging apps and phone makers build the model directly into their products.
