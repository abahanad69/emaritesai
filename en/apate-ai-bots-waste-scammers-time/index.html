Apate's 350,000 AI bots keep phone scammers talking for hours

The Australian company diverts scam calls and texts to AI personas that never pay, and collects intelligence.

2026-10-11 · EmaritesAI

An Australian company called Apate is using AI to waste scammers' time at scale. Its system diverts phone scammers onto calls with AI bots built to keep the conversation going as long as possible while never actually falling for the scam, founder and CEO Dali Kaafar told Wired's Kernel Panic newsletter this week.

Kaafar said the platform, used by banks and supported by telecom companies, runs around 350,000 bots. Beyond phone calls, they reply to scam text messages and join scam chat groups online. According to Kaafar, they have collected more than 250,000 pieces of information about fraudsters in real time, including scam links, money mule accounts and bank details.

The bots are given different personalities, language skills and profiles so scammers are less likely to notice they are talking to software. Kaafar said calls with them regularly run for more than two hours. His argument is simple arithmetic: every minute a scammer spends on a bot is a minute not spent dialling real people, and automated diallers mean that minute can represent hundreds of potential victims.

Wired's reporters tested a demo in which they played the scammer. They spent six minutes trying to sell the bot a cryptocurrency investment and did not succeed.

Apate's website lists voice, text and intelligence products aimed at telecom operators, financial services, crypto firms and government agencies. The bot and data figures come from the company and have not been independently audited.
