Amex launches a check so AI agents buy only what you approved

American Express's Intent Intelligence Framework records a shopper's instructions before an AI agent pays and ties the payment to them.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

American Express has introduced the Intent Intelligence Framework, a set of tools meant to stop AI shopping agents from buying things their users never asked for. Amex told TechCrunch it hopes the approach becomes an industry standard.

The process starts with a Create Intent API. When a customer asks an agent to buy something, the API records the request together with limits such as the merchant, a maximum budget and product preferences. The customer then confirms their identity and approves that captured intent.

Amex then issues an Intent ID and a Proof of Intent Token that carries the customer's limits. The agent uses the token to get a secure digital payment credential for checkout, so it never handles the real card number. Hannah Bozian, Amex's vice president of agentic partnerships and strategy, gave the example of a customer who asks for white Nike sneakers in size 9: if the agent tries to buy Adidas, Amex could stop the purchase.

The same ID and token can later help with fraud checks and disputes. Amex said it has not yet seen more disputes from agent purchases but wants protections in place as this kind of shopping grows. In its own Trendex survey, 66% of US consumers said they are likely to use AI agents to find the best price in the next 12 months, and 61% said they would use them for recommendations.

The framework joins Amex's Agentic Commerce Experiences developer kit, which launched in April.

3 things to know
  1. Amex records a shopper's limits before an AI agent is allowed to pay
  2. Agents pay with a token-based credential, never the real card number
  3. 66% of surveyed US consumers may use agents to find the best price
Sources: TechCrunch, American Express ACE developer kit press release (Nasdaq)
