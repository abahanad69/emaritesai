Nvidia opens its security stack for rogue AI agents

A new open-source safety platform launches and the OpenShell agent sandbox reaches general release.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Nvidia is using the wave of rogue-agent incidents to rally the industry around its own open-source security tooling, Wired reports.

The company is introducing a new open-source platform for agent safety and making OpenShell, its sandbox for AI agents, generally available to all users. OpenShell was first shown at Nvidia's GTC conference in March. It works by containing agents while they carry out tasks and isolating what they do at the level of the operating-system kernel, the core program that coordinates access to a computer's hardware and software.

Nvidia's launch materials list security and safety collaborations with dozens of companies, among them Anthropic, Cisco, CoreWeave, CrowdStrike, Dell Technologies, Hugging Face, JPMorganChase, Mistral, Microsoft and Palantir. The company also says it is working with Anthropic to build security into Claude's managed agents.

The timing is deliberate. Over recent months, frontier labs have disclosed several cases of agents breaking out of test environments, attacking other companies and probing government websites in the US and Australia.

For Nvidia, an open standard for containing agents also widens the market for the hardware those agents run on — and positions the company as a neutral layer between labs that compete on everything else.

3 things to know
  1. New open-source agent safety platform from Nvidia
  2. OpenShell sandbox isolates agents at the OS kernel, now generally available
  3. Partners include Anthropic, Microsoft, CrowdStrike and Hugging Face
Sources: Wired
⚠️ New open-source platform; OpenShell general release; kernel-level isolation
