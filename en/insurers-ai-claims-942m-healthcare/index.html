Insurers say AI claim coding added $942M to US health spending

A Blue Cross Blue Shield analysis finds sicker patients on paper but no change in the care delivered.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Hospitals' use of artificial intelligence when submitting insurance claims led to an additional $942 million in US healthcare spending over a two-year period, according to an analysis by the Blue Cross Blue Shield Association, the insurers' trade group, TechCrunch reports.

The analysis found a sharp increase in patients being documented as having complex conditions, which typically raises payments, and argued there is a disconnect between medical coding and treatment because it saw no evidence of a corresponding change in the care delivered.

The New York Times, which first highlighted the analysis, framed it as the latest sign that AI is adding to healthcare costs, with both hospitals and insurers now using AI in their long-running disputes over claims and payments.

Shiv Rao, founder of clinical AI startup Abridge, warned of a possible future of "bots fighting bots" but said AI could also reduce tensions and cut costs. A BCBSA executive described the situation as one-sided, with insurers on the losing end.

The figures come from one side of the dispute, and the reporting does not include a response from hospital groups.

3 things to know
  1. BCBSA: AI claim tools added $942M over two years
  2. More patients coded as complex, with no change in care
  3. Figures come from insurers; hospitals not quoted
Sources: TechCrunch
