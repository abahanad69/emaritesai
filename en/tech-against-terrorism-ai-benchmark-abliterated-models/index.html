81 of 134 AI models fail a counter-terrorism safety test

Tech Against Terrorism also found that copies of open models with safeguards stripped out now appear online within three days.

2026-10-15 · EmaritesAI

A new benchmark from Tech Against Terrorism, a UK nonprofit, suggests most AI models can still be pushed into helping extremists. The group's Counter-Terrorism AI Benchmark report, shared with Wired before publication, tested 134 models from US, Chinese and European developers, including OpenAI, xAI, Anthropic, Meta and Google.

Researchers sent each model more than 200 prompts on subjects such as mass-casualty attacks and weapons, three times each: once posing as a terrorist, once as someone working in secret and once as a safety researcher. Only two models passed completely. Eighty-one failed, meaning they gave at least one complete answer on a mass-casualty subject or scored below 90 out of 100. Most closed models from large companies did well overall, the report found. Claiming to be a researcher, with no proof, made models almost nine times as likely to answer, and they did so 16.9% of the time.

The finding the group calls most worrying concerns open-weight models whose safety training has been removed, a process known as abliteration. Such copies now appear online less than three days after a new open model is released on average, compared with more than 24 days in 2024. Meta's Llama 3.1 8B scored 97 on the benchmark, while a stripped version scored 3.

Executive director Adam Hadley said policymakers focus on long-term risks while paying little attention to these modified models. The report adds to pressure on open-model developers to find safeguards that cannot be removed easily.

  3. Guardrail-free copies of open models now appear in under 3 days
