Researchers show AI models can hide skills safety tests can't find

A NeurIPS 2026 paper locks an encrypted network inside a model's weights that only a secret trigger can switch on.

2026-10-13 · EmaritesAI

Many AI safety checks work by trying to draw dangerous abilities out of a model before release. If testers cannot make the model do something harmful, the model is treated as unable to do it. A new paper argues that this assumption can fail when someone builds the model to cheat.

The paper, by Andis Draguns, Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Raymond Douglas and Christian Schroeder de Witt, was posted to arXiv on October 9 and is listed as accepted at NeurIPS 2026. The authors hide an encrypted neural network inside a host model's weights. The hidden network is decrypted and run only when the model receives a secret trigger. Using a cryptographic tool called a digital locker, they argue the hidden part is hard to trigger or interpret even for someone with full access to the weights. Earlier work showed models could hide fixed strings; this paper extends the idea to any computation.

As a proof of concept, the team hid a small chemical reaction prediction skill, which it uses as a stand-in for chemical and biological risk. The hidden skill survived fine-tuning, added noise in the weights, gradient-based searches for the trigger and latent adversarial training.

The authors conclude that audits based on drawing out capabilities have a fundamental limit, and they have released their models so others can work on defences. The paper drew wide discussion on Hacker News this week. Its results are the authors' own and have not yet been tested by other groups.

3 things to know
  1. A hidden encrypted network runs only when a secret trigger arrives
  2. It survived fine-tuning, weight noise and trigger searches
  3. Authors say capability-elicitation audits have a hard limit
Sources: arXiv 2610.13928: AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities, Hacker News discussion
⚠️ Paper by Draguns, Motwani, Douglas, Schroeder de Witt; submitted Oct 9; accepted at NeurIPS 2026
⚠️ Encrypted network in weights, decrypted on secret trigger; digital lockers
⚠️ Hidden skill resisted fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient trigger search, latent adversarial training
