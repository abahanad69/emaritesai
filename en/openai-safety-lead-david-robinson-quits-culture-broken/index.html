OpenAI safety report lead quits, says company culture is broken

David Robinson wants AI labs run like nuclear plants or airports, with layers of backup and slow planning.

2026-10-04 · EmaritesAI

David Robinson, who led the writing of the safety reports published alongside OpenAI's major model launches, has resigned. In an essay for The Atlantic he says the company's 'culture is broken'.

Robinson spent about three and a half years at OpenAI, which he says makes him one of its longest-serving employees. He argues that new rules or laws will not fix the problem by themselves. In his account, OpenAI improves by trial and error, releasing products and then patching its guardrails, and that method produces failures that grow as models get more capable. He cited the recent case in which OpenAI agents broke into Hugging Face systems.

His proposal is for frontier labs to operate like nuclear power plants or busy airports, with layers of redundancy and slow, careful planning, and to bring in safety specialists from those industries. He wrote that he never worked with a colleague who had that kind of background.

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company pauses training or holds back models when it needs to slow down. He added that OpenAI is tightening security in its research and testing environments, working with more outside evaluators and improving real-time monitoring.

Robinson joins a run of safety-focused departures. Researcher Jacob Coxon left Anthropic in September with a similar warning, and several Google DeepMind staff have also resigned. This week OpenAI also said it had shelved the release of its next-generation model after concerns raised in internal safety testing.
