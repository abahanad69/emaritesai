Pentagon seeks $30M for AI-powered 'Polygraph Next' lie detector

The five-year program would add machine-learning scoring and contact-free 'standoff' sensing.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

The US government wants to spend $30.3 million over the next five years on an improved lie detector, according to a Department of Defense budget request reported by MIT Technology Review.

The program, called "Polygraph+" or "Polygraph Next", will focus on scoring algorithms that use AI and machine learning. It will also develop "standoff sensing", a technique for taking physiological readings from a person without attaching a device to them.

The stated aim is to improve the accuracy and reliability of polygraph assessments. Traditional polygraphs measure signals such as heart rate and breathing through sensors attached to the body, and their reliability has long been contested.

MIT Technology Review notes that the project could be the latest in a long line of failed attempts to use technology to detect lies. Contact-free sensing would also raise new questions about when and where people could be assessed without their knowledge.

3 things to know
  1. $30.3M requested over five years
  2. AI and machine-learning scoring of polygraph data
  3. 'Standoff sensing' reads signals without contact
Sources: MIT Technology Review
