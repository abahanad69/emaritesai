Research with AI, then check every source in 5 steps

AI assistants are fast at finding and summarising information, and you still need to confirm what they cite.

2026-10-08 · EmaritesAI

AI assistants with web search can gather sources and draft a summary of a topic in minutes. The weak point is the step between the source and the summary: a model can attach a real link to a claim the page does not make, or round a number the wrong way.

This week gave a reminder from mathematics. OpenAI withdrew three papers from its collection of AI-produced proofs after a single sign error, and its README warns that results without a formal check may still contain errors. Everyday research has no automatic checker, so the checking falls to you.

Start by asking the assistant to tie every claim to a specific source. Then open the links yourself and confirm that each page exists and says what the summary says. Give more weight to primary sources, such as a company announcement, a paper or official statistics, than to articles that summarise them. Check each number against a second, independent source before you use it. Finally, look at the date, because prices, job titles and rules change.

Used this way, the AI does the gathering and you do the verifying, which is still much faster than starting from a blank search page.

Step by step
  1. Ask for sources: Tell the assistant to link each claim to a specific page you can open.
  2. Open the links: Confirm each page exists and actually says what the summary claims.
  3. Go primary: Prefer the company post, paper or official data over articles about it.
  4. Check numbers twice: Find every figure in a second, independent source before using it.
  5. Note the date: Make sure the source is current, since prices, roles and rules change.
3 things to know
  1. Ask the AI to tie each claim to a specific source you can open
  2. Open every link and confirm it says what the summary claims
  3. Check numbers in a second source and make sure facts are current
