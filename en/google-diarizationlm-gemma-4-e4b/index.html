Google releases a 4B model that fixes who-said-what in transcripts

DiarizationLM-Gemma-4-E4B cleans up speaker labels in speech transcripts and runs locally as a 5.3GB download.

2026-10-05 · EmaritesAI

Google has released DiarizationLM-Gemma-4-E4B-v1, a small open model that fixes a common flaw in speech transcripts: text attributed to the wrong speaker. The task is called speaker diarization, working out who spoke when.

The model does not transcribe audio itself. It takes the output of a speech recognition and diarization system, with speaker tags, and corrects the tags, especially around short turns and interjections where words often land on the wrong person.

It is built on Google's Gemma 4 E4B, a 4-billion-parameter model, and was fine-tuned on four standard corpora: Fisher and Callhome telephone calls, and the ICSI and AMI meeting recordings. Google says it is half the size of its earlier 8B DiarizationLM model.

According to the model card, the word diarization error rate on Fisher fell to 2.99% from a 5.32% baseline, and on Callhome to 4.92% from 7.74%. Gains on the multi-speaker meeting sets were smaller: ICSI moved from 14.70% to 14.10%. These are Google's own measurements.

The weights are on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license, including 4-bit GGUF files of about 5.3GB for llama.cpp and Ollama. The full 16-bit version is about 16GB. The card notes it is not an officially supported Google product.

For anyone building meeting notes or call analytics, cleaner speaker labels mean action items land on the right name.
