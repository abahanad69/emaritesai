Ataraxos AI beats Stratego's top player 15-1, trained on 16 GPUs

A university team cracked a game of hidden information that had resisted AI, for a few thousand dollars.

2026-10-03 · EmaritesAI

Chess and Go fell to machines years ago. Stratego, a board game built on hidden information and bluffing, held out. Now a team from MIT, Carnegie Mellon, New York University and Stanford says its AI system, Ataraxos, has reached superhuman level, in a paper published in Nature on September 30.

In a 20-game match, Ataraxos beat Pim Niemeijer, described by the researchers as the most decorated Stratego player ever, with 15 wins, one loss and four draws. MIT says the system also went 39-2 against top players at the Stratego world championship.

What makes the result notable is cost. The team reports that training took one week on 16 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, a few thousand dollars, and roughly one five-hundredth of the compute used by earlier industrial efforts. According to the paper, Ataraxos plays more strongly than DeepNash, the 2022 system from Google DeepMind, while using under one-hundredth of its training examples.

Stratego is hard because each player sees where enemy pieces are but not what they are, and a game can run to thousands of moves. Ataraxos combines a network trained through self-play, a model that guesses hidden pieces, and search at decision time.

The same method set strong results in Barrage Stratego, the card game Hanabi and the Chinese game dou dizhu, suggesting uses beyond board games.
