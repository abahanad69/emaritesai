Anthropic's new rules ban cruel abuse of Claude and election deception

The first usage policy update in over a year also covers weapons software, tracking people without consent and fake influence campaigns.

2026-10-09 · EmaritesAI

Anthropic has published its first usage policy update in more than a year. The new rules take effect on 12 November.

The change getting the most attention is a ban on abusing Claude. Anthropic says it targets repeated, cruel behaviour toward the model with no apparent purpose, in extreme cases only. Ordinary frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, and testing or research are not covered. Ending persistently abusive conversations on Claude.ai and Claude Code remains the main way the company enforces it.

A new section on deceptive campaigns gathers rules that were spread across several parts of the old policy. It covers hiding who is behind a message, boosting content with fake accounts and building tools for influence operations, whether political or commercial. The elections section now focuses on deceiving voters or disrupting elections, and drops the blanket ban on personalised campaign targeting.

The weapons ban now explicitly includes software and components that make weapons work, and arming drones or other autonomous vehicles. Claude also cannot be used to track people without consent, or to decide who to investigate, arrest or charge.

For uses that affect health, finances or legal rights, a qualified human must still be able to review and override Claude's recommendations.

3 things to know
  1. New Anthropic usage policy takes effect on 12 November
  2. It bans repeated, cruel abuse of Claude in extreme cases only
  3. Weapons ban now covers software and arming drones
