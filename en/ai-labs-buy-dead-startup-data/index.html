AI labs pay failed startups for their old code, chats and emails

Shutdown brokers now sell code and workplace data from closing companies as training material, and privacy lawyers are uneasy.

2026-10-13 · EmaritesAI

When a startup shuts down, its code, Slack messages, emails and project files used to be worth little. AI companies are changing that, according to a report published on October 13 by MIT Technology Review. Labs and the firms that build simulated workplaces for training AI agents want realistic records of how people work, and closing companies have them.

The biggest deal in the report involves Spirit Airlines. Google paid $10 million for the airline's data, which included 100 million emails, 500 million Teams messages and records on 176,000 employees. Spirit's flight attendants' union objected on privacy grounds. The report also says Google outbid the AI data company Mercor for that data, and that SpaceX's AI unit has weighed buying data from struggling startups, citing Bloomberg.

Smaller deals are becoming routine. SimpleClosure, which helps startups wind down, says it has handled more than 150 deals and that 47% of its deals now include code or data sales. One founder told the magazine he received about $25,000 for his company's code base. Sellers say they remove personal details and leave out HR records, and buyers say they clean the data too.

Lawyers quoted in the piece are cautious. Emails and calendars can contain doctor visits or school pickup times, and Emory University law professor Ifeoma Ajunwa says no US federal law bans such sales. The money from them usually goes to creditors and investors, not to the employees whose messages are sold.
