USA Today Co. sues OpenAI, seeking more than $250 million

The publisher says OpenAI copied hundreds of thousands of articles from 19 of its papers to train and run its models.

2026-10-09 · EmaritesAI

USA Today Co. has become the latest news publisher to take OpenAI to court. In a suit filed on Thursday in a New York federal court, the company and 13 affiliated entities accuse OpenAI of copying hundreds of thousands of articles from 19 publications, including USA Today, The Tennessean and the Detroit Free Press, to train and operate its models.

The publisher asks for damages of more than $250 million. Its claim rests on statutory damages of up to $150,000 for each work it says was infringed wilfully, plus up to $25,000 for each case where copyright information was allegedly removed. The filing also asks the court to order the destruction of GPT models and training sets built with its content, and requests a jury trial.

Beyond training, the complaint argues that ChatGPT repackages the papers' reporting for its users, which the publisher says draws readers away from its sites. It also alleges OpenAI used programs designed to strip copyright management information from articles.

OpenAI did not immediately comment, according to The Verge and Forbes. The company already faces similar suits from The New York Times, Ziff Davis, The Intercept, Encyclopaedia Britannica and a group of nearly 400 local newspapers. None of these cases has yet produced a final ruling on whether training AI on news articles is fair use under US law.
