How to run an AI model on your own laptop, in 4 steps

Free tools let you chat with open models offline, keeping your data on your device.

2026-10-03 · EmaritesAI

Most people use AI through a website, which means every prompt travels to someone else's servers. Running a model on your own machine changes that: your documents stay with you, it works offline and there is no monthly fee.

This week showed the trend from two sides. Ai2 released AstaBrief, an 8-billion-parameter research model that institutions can run themselves, and NVIDIA announced a cheaper DGX Spark desktop built for local AI. But you can start with the laptop you already own.

First, check your memory. A model must fit in RAM, or in GPU or unified memory, so a machine with 16GB can comfortably handle small models of a few billion parameters, while larger ones need far more.

Second, install a free runner such as Ollama or LM Studio. These apps download models and give you a chat window or a local API.

Third, pick a small, compressed version of an open model. Many come in quantized form, which trades a little accuracy for a much smaller size.

Finally, test it on real tasks. Local models are usually less capable than top cloud systems, so use them for drafting, summarising and private notes, and check important facts elsewhere.

Step by step
  1. Check memory: The model must fit in your RAM or GPU memory, so note how much you have.
  2. Install a runner: Free apps like Ollama or LM Studio download and run models for you.
  3. Pick a small model: Choose a compressed (quantized) open model of a few billion parameters.
  4. Test and verify: Use it for drafts and summaries, and double-check key facts.
3 things to know
  1. Local AI keeps your data on your device and works offline
  2. Match model size to your RAM; small quantized models suit most laptops
  3. Local models are less capable, so verify important answers
Sources: Ollama documentation, LM Studio documentation, Ai2 - AstaBrief, NVIDIA Blog - DGX Spark 64GB
