OpenAI and Anthropic tell UK MPs that AI safety rules must be binding

Google DeepMind and Meta declined to back new legislation at the same parliamentary hearing, where officials admitted model testing is voluntary.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

Four frontier AI companies gave evidence to a cross-party committee of British MPs on October 13, and they split into two camps over regulation.

Anthropic's head of Europe, Liam Booth-Smith, said the company has long argued for rules that companies must follow, telling MPs, "The rules should be binding." OpenAI vice president Tom Duff Gordon backed a statutory framework and an international convention, and said mandatory incident reporting would be a sensible first step, according to WIRED.

Google DeepMind and Meta did not join them. DeepMind's Owen Larter said the company would focus on standards, while Meta's Rob Sherman pointed MPs to the regulators Britain already has.

Committee chair Liam Byrne said the session was meant to put those differences on the record and show that self-regulation cannot be relied on. Officials then confirmed how little the current system requires. Henry de Zoete, director of the AI Security Institute, said the institute depends on companies and foreign governments choosing to share their models before release. Asked whether the whole regime was voluntary, Cabinet Office permanent secretary Cat Little agreed that it is, for now.

On the same day, MI5 director general Ken McCallum used his annual threat update to name AI's potential to help with biological weapons as the misuse risk he weighs most heavily, while saying it has not yet happened.

The committee has not said when it will publish its findings. The UK still has no AI-specific law for frontier models.

  3. Officials said pre-release testing of AI models is voluntary for now
