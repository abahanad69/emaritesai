100+ rural US data centres could qualify for a new tax break

Expanded opportunity zone rules take effect on 1 January, but Microsoft, Meta and Amazon say they are not using them.

2026-10-05 · EmaritesAI

A tax incentive meant for low-income areas could soon help pay for AI data centres in the US countryside, according to research reported by WIRED.

From 1 January, projects in designated rural census tracts become eligible for expanded opportunity zone benefits under last year's One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The Searchlight Institute, a policy think tank, compared planned data centres with eligible tracts and found more than 100 rural projects in development that could qualify.

That count is conservative. It came from a database of under 700 planned or under-construction data centres, while other datasets put the number in development closer to 1,500. Separate research from Pew found that 13% of operating US data centres are in rural areas, but about 67% of planned ones are heading there.

Emily Kraschel, a tax analyst at Searchlight, told WIRED that the only requirement is capital investment, which does not guarantee local jobs. Data centres employ far fewer people than factories of a similar cost.

Microsoft, Meta and Amazon told WIRED they do not use the program for their data centres. Google did not respond. Companies must set up a special investment fund to claim the benefit, and because the data is confidential IRS information, it is hard to know who uses it.

Senator Josh Hawley has introduced a bill to bar opportunity zone funding for data centres, as local opposition to new sites grows in several states.

  2. Searchlight found 100+ rural data centres in development that could qualify
  3. Microsoft, Meta and Amazon say they don't use the program
