ChatGPT images carry real New Yorker cartoonists' signatures

A Nieman Lab investigation found the names of more than 15 cartoonists on AI-made cartoons they never drew.

2026-10-06 · EmaritesAI

ChatGPT's image generator has been placing the signatures of real New Yorker cartoonists on cartoons they did not draw, according to an investigation published by Nieman Lab.

The report documents more than 15 cartoonists whose pen names appeared on AI-made images, collected from online forums and from the reporter's own tests. One widely shared cartoon, made after a user asked ChatGPT for a cartoon in the magazine's style, carried the signature of cartoonist Brendan Loper. He told Nieman Lab he received messages from people asking whether it was his work. "My name is my name," he said.

For cartoonists, a signature works as proof of authorship, since their work is often shared without credit. Emily Flake, another contributor whose pen name appeared on ChatGPT images, compared it to having a quote attributed to her that she never said.

An OpenAI spokesperson said the company appreciates people flagging bugs and unintended behaviour in its models. After OpenAI was notified, ChatGPT began warning that New Yorker-style prompts may break its rules on similarity to third-party content. Nieman Lab found it still signed some images with real names at publication.

Condé Nast, The New Yorker's owner, signed a licensing deal with OpenAI in 2024. A New Yorker spokesperson told Nieman Lab the company has never allowed an AI developer to train on its cartoons. OpenAI declined to say how signatures entered its training data.
