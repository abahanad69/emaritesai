PC shipments fall 20% in Q3 as memory prices hit demand

IDC calls it the biggest third-quarter drop it has recorded; Omdia says the market fell 21.2%.

2026-10-10 · EmaritesAI

The PC market had its sharpest fall in more than three years in the third quarter, as rising memory prices pushed buyers to purchase early and then hold back.

IDC says global shipments fell 20.1% from a year earlier to 62.7 million units, down from 78.5 million in the third quarter of 2025. It is the biggest third-quarter decline IDC has recorded. Shipments were also 9.1% below the second quarter's 68.2 million, although the third quarter usually beats the second.

Omdia, which counts slightly differently, puts the fall at 21.2% to 58.1 million laptops, desktops and workstations, its sharpest decline since early 2023. Desktop shipments dropped 23.5% to 11.7 million and laptops fell 20.6% to 46.4 million, according to Omdia.

IDC says many PC makers and retailers bought heavily earlier in the year to get ahead of memory price increases, which left little demand for the quarter. Jitesh Ubrani, research director at IDC, said sales channels now worry about holding too much stock in a market where high prices are keeping buyers away.

IDC notes that larger declines were recorded at the end of 2022 and the start of 2023, at minus 28.2% and minus 28.7%, but those came after the pandemic buying peak.

3 things to know
  1. IDC: Q3 PC shipments fell 20.1% to 62.7 million units
  2. It is the biggest third-quarter drop IDC has recorded
  3. Buyers stocked up early ahead of memory price rises
