Google lets anyone check media for its SynthID AI watermark

The free site checks images, video and audio from Google and partners including OpenAI, Nvidia and Kakao.

2026-10-08 · EmaritesAI

Google has opened its SynthID Detector to everyone. The website lets people upload an image, a video or an audio clip and check whether it carries SynthID, the invisible watermark Google adds to media made with its AI tools. Until now the detector was limited to selected journalists, media professionals and researchers.

The check covers content from Google's own models, including Nano Banana, Veo and Lyria, and from partners that use SynthID: OpenAI, Nvidia and Kakao, with Apple support coming soon, according to Google. The Next Web reports that the site is free, available worldwide in English, and requires a Google, OpenAI or Apple account to use.

Google says it has watermarked more than 180 billion images and videos since 2023, plus 240,000 years of audio. SynthID checks are also built into Search, the Gemini app and Chrome, which Google says handle more than one million requests a day.

The tool has clear limits. It only finds SynthID, so AI content from tools that do not use the watermark will not be flagged. Watermarks can sometimes be stripped, and a positive result says a file was made or edited with Google AI, not how much of it.

3 things to know
  1. Google's SynthID Detector is now open to everyone for free
  2. It checks images, video and audio from Google and partners
  3. It only finds SynthID, so other AI content goes undetected
