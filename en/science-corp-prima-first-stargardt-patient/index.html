Science Corp implants PRIMA vision chip in first Stargardt patient

Founder Max Hodak said the patient read a word by his second rehabilitation session, in a trial at Sydney Eye Hospital.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

Science Corporation has put its PRIMA vision implant into its first patient with Stargardt disease, according to CEO Max Hodak, who co-founded Neuralink before starting Science in 2021. He described the case on stage at TechCrunch Disrupt on October 13.

Stargardt disease is inherited. It gradually destroys the light-sensing cells at the centre of the retina and leaves a blind spot that grows over time. About 150,000 people worldwide have lost vision to it, according to TechCrunch, and there is no approved cure.

The patient received the implant in May as part of an ongoing trial at Sydney Eye Hospital in Australia. Hodak said he recognised a letter in his first rehabilitation session and read a word in the second.

PRIMA is a 2 by 2 millimetre chip placed under the retina in a procedure of about an hour. The patient wears glasses with a camera that sends images to the chip, which stimulates the remaining retinal cells in place of the lost photoreceptors. Hodak said the result is a small, sharp, black-and-white patch of central vision, "like looking through a straw."

The device is already sold in Europe for geographic atrophy, an advanced form of age-related macular degeneration. Science launched it commercially there in July and received FDA Humanitarian Use Device designations the same month. Hodak said a next-generation implant with a wider field of view should reach human trials within a year. These are early results from one patient, and no trial data has been published for Stargardt.
