Volantis raises $88M to link AI chips and memory with light

The San Francisco startup, co-founded by Micron's former HBM lead, plans first deliveries in 2027.

2026-10-04 · EmaritesAI

San Francisco chip startup Volantis has raised $88M in a Series A round co-led by investor Lachy Groom and Abstract Ventures, the company announced on October 1. Its total funding now stands at $97M. Other backers include John Doerr, VXI Capital, Triatomic and Susa Ventures.

Volantis is working on one of the main limits in AI hardware: moving data between processors and memory fast enough. Today that link is made with electrical wiring that only reaches a few millimetres. Volantis uses optical waveguides and tiny lasers instead, and says light lets memory sit more than 200 millimetres from the chip, so far more of it can be attached.

The company's planned A-1 inference system targets up to 10,000 tokens per second for each user on very large models. Reports differ on the model size it aims to support, with some citing more than 20 trillion parameters and others more than 10 trillion. Volantis says one version of its technology has been taped out, and it plans to deliver its first systems to customers in 2027.

The startup is led by CEO Tapa Ghosh and CTO Roy Meade, who previously led Micron's high-bandwidth memory programme and was a vice president at optical chip firm Ayar Labs.

The round comes as the AI boom strains global memory supply, pushing up prices for everything from servers to consumer gadgets.

3 things to know
  1. Volantis raised an $88M Series A, bringing total funding to $97M.
  2. It uses light instead of wires to connect AI processors to more memory.
  3. First A-1 inference systems are planned for customers in 2027.
