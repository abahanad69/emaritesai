Google pauses open source bug bounty over flood of AI reports

Google says most automated submissions to its open source rewards program were invalid and promises an update in early 2027.

2026-10-05 · EmaritesAI

Google has frozen its Open Source Software Vulnerability Rewards Program, which pays researchers for finding security flaws in open source projects the company maintains. The pause took effect on 1 October, and Google says it will give an update in the first quarter of 2027.

In a notice on the program site and on X, Google blamed a significant rise in automated submissions, "the vast majority of which are not valid." Tom's Hardware reports that Google engineers and open source maintainers were swamped by reports that were wrong or described problems that did not exist, a pattern typical of text generated by AI tools.

Researchers who want to keep reporting are being pointed to Google's other bug bounty programs, which remain open.

Google launched the open source program in August 2023, with rewards of up to $31,337 for the most serious findings in projects such as Bazel, Angular and Go.

The problem is not new. In January, the maintainers of curl, the widely used data transfer tool, ended their own bug bounty to remove the cash incentive for low-quality AI reports. Security researchers have warned for more than a year that cheap, plausible-looking reports cost maintainers hours to check and can bury real vulnerabilities.

The pause is notable because Google also sells AI tools that find bugs. It now has to decide how to reward genuine human and AI-assisted findings without paying for noise.

3 things to know
  1. Google's open source bug bounty has been paused since 1 October
  2. Google says most of the surge in automated reports was invalid
  3. An update is promised in the first quarter of 2027
Sources: TechCrunch, Tom's Hardware (via AI Weekly), Google Security Blog (2023 launch), The Register (curl)
