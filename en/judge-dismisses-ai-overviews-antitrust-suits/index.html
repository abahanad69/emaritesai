US judge throws out publisher lawsuits over Google's AI Overviews

Chegg and Penske Media failed to show an antitrust violation, the court ruled.

2026-10-02 · EmaritesAI

A federal judge in Washington has dismissed two antitrust lawsuits accusing Google of using its AI search summaries to take publishers' work and their readers. The cases were brought by education company Chegg and Penske Media Corporation, which owns Rolling Stone.

Both companies argued that Google abused its search dominance by effectively forcing sites to let their content feed AI Overviews, or risk vanishing from results, and that the summaries pulled visitors away from their pages. US District Judge Amit Mehta, who ruled in 2024 that Google held an illegal search monopoly, rejected that framing.

In his ruling, Mehta found the publishers had shown only an expectation that Google would send them traffic in return for free access to their content, and wrote that an expectation is not an agreement. He added that the court is not unsympathetic to publishers' situation, but said antitrust law cannot stand in for lawmakers deciding how to handle the economic effects of new technology.

The decision is a setback for media companies that hoped courts would force Google to pay for AI use of their content. It follows earlier dismissals of similar cases from smaller publishers, according to Press Gazette. Publishers' trade groups responded by calling for regulators to step in.

The pressure on Google is not over. Reports say it is now paying around 100 publishers in a pilot programme linked to AI Overviews and Gemini.

3 things to know
  1. Judge Amit Mehta dismissed antitrust suits by Chegg and Penske Media
  2. Ruling: expecting search traffic is not an agreement with Google
  3. Publishers' groups now urge regulators, not courts, to act
