Robotics startups raise $48B this year and spend it filming real work

PitchBook data cited by the Financial Times shows physical AI investors paying for videos of people doing everyday tasks.

2026-10-12 · EmaritesAI

Investors have put close to $48 billion into robotics and physical AI companies so far this year, the Financial Times reported on October 11, citing PitchBook data. The paper's focus was where that money is going: more of it is being spent on training data that cannot be scraped from the internet.

Language models learned from text that already existed online. Robots need examples of movement, touch and force in real homes, offices and factories, and that footage has to be commissioned and labelled. According to the FT, companies including Physical Intelligence, Skild AI, NEURA Robotics and Generalist are combining outside data vendors with their own collection teams. Methods include robot gyms, where workers guide robotic arms through tasks, and cameras placed in workplaces to film people doing ordinary jobs.

NEURA Robotics calls high-quality real-world data the scarcest resource in physical AI. The German company is building what it calls NEURA Gyms with RWTH Aachen University and the Technical University of Munich, and plans five sites across Europe, the US and China to be running by the end of 2026.

The pace of funding matches PitchBook's earlier figures. Its second-quarter report counted a record $18.6 billion across 450 robotics and physical AI deals, helped by a $5 billion round for defence company Anduril.

3 things to know
  1. Physical AI firms raised nearly $48B in 2026 so far, per PitchBook
  2. Startups are paying to film people at work to train robots
  3. NEURA plans five robot training gyms by the end of 2026
