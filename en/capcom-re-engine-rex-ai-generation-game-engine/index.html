Capcom plans to turn its RE Engine into an AI-era game engine

The Resident Evil maker says AI will speed up development, but AI-generated assets stay out of its games.

2026-10-04 · EmaritesAI

Capcom, the Japanese publisher behind Resident Evil and Street Fighter 6, has laid out plans to build AI into the engine that runs its biggest games.

At the Capcom Open Conference RE:2026 on October 2, programmer Satoshi Ishida presented the REX project. Its goal is to evolve the in-house RE Engine step by step into what he called an 'AI-generation game engine', working toward a future where the studio creates games together with AI, according to IGN's translation reported by The Verge.

Ishida said modern games have grown so large that even routine tasks take a long time, and bigger teams need easier tools. Capcom plans to improve the existing engine rather than start again, covering development speed, performance, tools and quality assurance. It also wants to unify its systems around a common programming language that suits both developers and AI, and to release some improvements as open source.

Capcom has said before that it will not put AI-generated assets into its games. The company says it is exploring AI in graphics, sound and programming to make production more efficient. The presentation did not give a timeline or say which AI models the engine would use.

The plan puts Capcom alongside other large studios testing AI in their pipelines, at a time when many players and developers remain wary of generated content in games.

3 things to know
  1. Capcom's REX project will gradually rebuild RE Engine around AI tools.
  2. AI will be used in graphics, sound and programming to speed development.
  3. Capcom says it will not put AI-generated assets into its games.
Sources: The Verge, Gamereactor, TheGamer
