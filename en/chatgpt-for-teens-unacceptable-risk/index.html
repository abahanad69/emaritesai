Common Sense Media rates ChatGPT for Teens an 'unacceptable risk'

The nonprofit says some safeguards worked but crisis responses fell short; OpenAI says the tests ran before parental controls were fully active.

2026-10-08 · EmaritesAI

Common Sense Media, a US nonprofit that reviews apps and media for families, has rated ChatGPT for Teens an "unacceptable risk". OpenAI launched the teen version in August with extra guardrails for users under 18.

According to TechCrunch's account of the report, some protections worked, including refusals of sexual roleplay. The product failed three of the five severe harms the researchers treat as red lines, which they linked to weak responses to teens in crisis. When the danger came from another person, ChatGPT pointed the teen to a trusted adult in 94% of crisis prompts. When the concern was the teen's reliance on ChatGPT itself, it rarely did. Across nearly 2,000 prompts, the testers saw only two break reminders.

OpenAI said the testing did not reflect how its safeguards work in practice, and argued that much of it began and ended before parental controls were fully switched on. The company says teens use ChatGPT for under 15 minutes a day on average and that fewer than 2% stay for more than three hours in a row.

On the same day OpenAI announced flashcards, quizzes and a College Planner for ChatGPT for Teens, and said it is funding a student advisory council at Boston Children's Hospital's Digital Wellness Lab for three years.
