Nvidia opens its Vera Rubin rack designs to the Open Compute Project

The contributions include a rack spec for up to 250kW and a grid rule, written with Google, Meta and Microsoft, for AI sites to recover within two seconds.

2026-10-13 · EmaritesAI

Nvidia used the Open Compute Project Global Summit in San Jose on October 12 to hand more of its data center hardware designs to the open community. The main contribution is the rack and compute tray design for Vera Rubin, its next AI platform, released through Nvidia's MGX architecture so other manufacturers can build compatible systems.

The new MGX 1.2 specification covers racks of up to 250 kilowatts, with blind-mate liquid cooling, a liquid-cooled 5,000-amp busbar and fanless liquid-cooled compute trays. Nvidia also published a Vera CPU rack design meant for the parts of agent work that run on CPUs, such as tool calls, orchestration and sandboxed code.

Two documents go beyond Nvidia's own hardware. An Open Data Center Specification, version 0.7, written with AMD, Google and Meta, covers facility design from power to cooling to layout. A Grid Resilience Specification, written with Google, Meta and Microsoft, says large AI sites should return to near-full load within two seconds after a grid disturbance. That matters to power utilities, which worry about huge, fast swings in demand from AI training.

On networking, Meta said it is adopting Nvidia's Spectrum-6 Ethernet switches, rated at 102.4 terabits per second. Nvidia and Cisco also proposed USFP, a pluggable optics format with a path to 400 gigabits per lane. All details come from Nvidia's announcement.

3 things to know
  1. Nvidia contributed Vera Rubin rack and tray designs to OCP
  2. MGX 1.2 spec supports liquid-cooled racks of up to 250kW
  3. New grid spec: AI sites back to near-full load within 2 seconds
