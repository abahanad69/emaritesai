5 ways to protect your family from AI voice-clone scams

A cloned voice can sound exactly like a relative. A few habits make the scam much harder to pull off.

2026-09-29 · EmaritesAI

AI tools can now imitate a person's voice from a short recording, and scammers use them to call victims pretending to be a family member in trouble. TechCrunch this week told the story of a founder whose grandfather paid a ransom after a cloned voice convinced him his brother had been kidnapped.

The losses are real. According to the FBI, Americans lost close to $900 million to AI-driven scams last year, up 24% from the year before, and people aged 60 and over lost twice as much as those aged 50 to 59.

The good news is that simple habits defeat most of these calls. The scam depends on panic, urgency and secrecy, so the defence is to slow down and verify through a channel the caller does not control.

Agree on a family code word that is never posted online. If a call sounds urgent, hang up and call the person back on the number you already have. Treat any request for gift cards, crypto or unusual transfers as a red flag. Keep social media accounts private where possible, since public videos provide voice samples. And talk to older relatives about this before a call ever comes.

If you are targeted, report it to the police and your bank immediately.

Step by step
  1. Family code word: Agree on a secret word for emergencies and never post it online.
  2. Hang up and call back: Ring the person on the number you already have, not the one calling you.
  3. Spot the red flags: Panic, secrecy and requests for gift cards, crypto or odd transfers.
  4. Share less voice: Keep social accounts private; public videos give scammers voice samples.
  5. Brief your elders: Talk to older relatives now, and report any attempt to police and your bank.
3 things to know
  1. AI can clone a voice from a short clip
  2. FBI: ~$900M lost to AI-driven scams in the US last year
  3. Verify on a known number before paying anything
