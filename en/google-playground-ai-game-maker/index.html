Google's Playground lets anyone build browser games from prompts

The Google Labs experiment turns typed descriptions into playable 2D or 3D games that can be shared or published to a gallery.

2026-10-08 · EmaritesAI

Google has launched Playground, an experimental platform from Google Labs for making, playing and sharing games without writing code. Users type what they want, from the rules to the characters and the look of the world, and can test the result straight away.

Creators can start from a blank canvas, remix starter prompts or pick a genre such as trivia or racing, according to TechCrunch. They can choose 2D or 3D and single-player or multiplayer, and upload their own pictures for the AI to turn into game assets in a matching style. Changes are made the same way, by asking.

Games run in the browser on phones and laptops. They can stay private, be shared by link or be published to the Playground Explore gallery, which ranks games using player ratings and play activity. Some genres support leaderboards and multiplayer, and Google says every published game goes through safety screening.

Google plans to connect Playground to Unity Spark, which it says will bring more advanced mechanics and higher-quality 3D. Unity Spark is still in testing, with a closed beta coming soon.

Playground is open to users in the US aged 18 and over at playground.google. Browsing and playing are free, while making games uses a weekly token allowance that is larger for Google AI subscribers. Roblox launched a similar prompt-based Build feature in July.

3 things to know
  1. Playground turns text prompts into 2D or 3D browser games
  2. Games can be kept private, shared by link or published to a gallery
  3. Open to US users 18+; Unity Spark integration is planned
