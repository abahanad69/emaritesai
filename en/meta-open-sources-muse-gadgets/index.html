Meta open-sources Muse Gadgets so makers can build AI devices

Firmware and a Linux SDK let hobbyists connect the Muse agent to ESP32 boards and Raspberry Pi.

2026-10-03 · EmaritesAI

Meta is inviting hardware tinkerers to build their own devices for Muse, its personal AI agent. The company has released Muse Gadgets, an open-source project made up of device firmware and a Linux software development kit.

With the tools, developers can program an off-the-shelf ESP32 board or set up a Raspberry Pi, then connect Muse to displays, buttons, sensors and motors. Meta's starter ideas include a colour e-ink screen that shows reminders and an HDMI stick that puts Muse on a TV. The company also opened a Discord channel for support and warns builders to proceed at their own risk.

Meta has built one gadget itself. Muse Home Link is a small USB-C device that joins a home network and, using community-built skills, can switch on lights, control a TV or send a file to a printer, depending on the setup. Nat Friedman, head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, said Meta made 5,000 units and is offering them free to Muse subscribers while supplies last, with shipping expected in the coming weeks.

The project is part of Meta's push to make Muse more than a chatbot, alongside recent moves into small business and enterprise tools. It arrives as Muse faces scrutiny over desktop permissions, after Apple this week announced tighter controls on Full Disk Access.

Meta has not said whether third-party gadgets will face any review.

3 things to know

  2. Works with ESP32 boards and Raspberry Pi, displays and sensors
  3. Meta made 5,000 Muse Home Link units, free to subscribers
