AI agents rebuild classic games' code, putting Halo in a browser tab

Browser ports of Halo: CE and GTA: Vice City spread as one developer reports 83% byte-exact code after 600B+ tokens.

2026-10-12 · EmaritesAI

AI coding agents are turning compiled games back into readable source code, and the results are spreading fast. Kotaku reported on October 9 that browser versions of Halo: Combat Evolved, Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, Skate 3, Call of Duty: Black Ops and The Simpsons: Hit and Run have appeared online, many traced to a post on October 8. The writer said several ran close to flawlessly and linked the wave to Claude Opus 5.5, which he described as very good at decompiling.

A detailed account from reverse engineer Maurice Heumann shows how this works. In a post the same day, he described using Claude Code and Codex agents over about three months to rebuild an unnamed first-person shooter in C++. At its peak the setup ran 16 agents, with Opus 5.5 among the models. He estimates the job used 600 to 700 billion tokens.

The first month produced a game that launched and loaded maps, but much of the code was wrong underneath. Progress came after he added a strict check that compares each rebuilt function with the original binary. He reports 99% of functions present and 83% byte-exact, and says he will not share the code.

The ports raise copyright questions that publishers have yet to answer in public. Kotaku's writer compared the situation to the early days of pirated DVDs.
