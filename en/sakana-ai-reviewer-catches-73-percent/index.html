Sakana's AI reviewer catches 73% of planted errors in core claims

A new benchmark plants 1,164 contradictions in 257 papers to test whether AI reviewers find mistakes instead of only mimicking humans.

2026-10-12 · EmaritesAI

Most tests of AI peer review ask how closely a model's comments resemble a human reviewer's. A paper from Sakana AI, posted to arXiv on October 8, asks a different question: can the reviewer find a mistake that someone deliberately put there?

To measure that, the team built a Contradiction Benchmark. They took 257 openly licensed papers from ACL, AISTATS, CVPR, ICML and NeurIPS and inserted 1,164 contradictions, some in a paper's central claim and others in details further away from it. An o3-based judge then checked whether each review flagged the planted error.

Their system, Multi-Layered Review, uses three agents built on off-the-shelf Claude models: one summarises the appendix, an optional one searches prior work, and a third reads the paper in three passes. Across four reviews it caught 73.43% of errors in core claims and 40.95% overall. The best earlier system, AgentReview, caught 14.81% of core-claim errors. On 211 papers that were later withdrawn from arXiv, it matched the real problem more often than the baselines.

Each review costs about $0.47 and needs no GPU or fine-tuning, according to the paper. The authors say the tool should support human reviewers rather than replace them, and note that hidden prompt injections in a paper could still sway every AI reviewer they tested.
