Snap a photo, get an answer: 4 tips for AI vision

Your phone camera is now one of the most useful ways to ask an AI a question.

2026-10-02 · EmaritesAI

Typing is no longer the only way to ask an AI for help. Assistants such as ChatGPT and Gemini can look at a photo or a live camera feed and answer questions about it. This week Google added Guided Vision to Gemini Live on Android, which describes what your camera sees aloud, and OpenAI launched a ChatGPT feature that shows clothes on you from your own photo.

Getting good answers is mostly about the picture and the question. A sharp, well-lit image of just the thing you care about beats a cluttered shot. A specific question, such as "What is the expiry date on this jar?", beats "What is this?". Follow-up questions work too, so you can narrow things down instead of starting again.

Know the limits. Vision models can misread small print, miscount objects or sound confident when they are wrong. Google itself warns that Guided Vision should not be used for navigation or obstacle detection. For medicine labels, contracts or anything safety-related, check the original yourself.

Finally, think before you share. Avoid photographing ID cards, bank details or other people without their consent, and check your assistant's privacy settings.

Step by step
  1. Frame it well: Fill the shot with the object, in good light, and hold the camera steady.
  2. Ask precisely: Name what you need, like the expiry date, the dosage or the plant's species.
  3. Follow up: Ask it to zoom in on a detail, compare options or explain its answer.
  4. Verify and protect: Check important answers yourself and never snap IDs or bank cards.
3 things to know
  1. A sharp, focused photo plus a specific question gets the best answers
  2. Use follow-up questions to narrow down instead of starting over
  3. Double-check anything medical, legal or safety-related yourself
