Microsoft unveils Maia 300, claims 40% more AI output per megawatt

The third-generation in-house chip will become the main system for running AI in Microsoft's products from 2027.

2026-10-13 · EmaritesAI

Microsoft announced Maia 300 on October 12, the third generation of its own AI chip. It is built for inference, the work of running trained models, and Microsoft says it is designed for long conversations, reasoning models and multi-step agents.

The compute parts are made on TSMC's N2P process and the chip is split into chiplets for compute, memory and data movement, with eight stacks of HBM4 memory. Microsoft quotes 18 terabytes per second of memory bandwidth. Its headline claim is 40% more tokens per megawatt than top GPU systems, though it did not name the systems it compared against.

A full rack holds 96 chips and draws about 167 kilowatts, according to Reuters, and delivers up to seven times the output of a Maia 200 rack. Rani Borkar, who leads Azure hardware systems and infrastructure, told Reuters the chip delivers more intelligence for every watt, dollar and byte of memory.

Microsoft says Maia will become the primary system for running AI in its products starting in 2027, with gigawatt-scale deployments. The Information reported in August that Microsoft was in talks with TSMC for more than 300,000 Maia 300 chips for that year. The current Maia 200 already serves Microsoft Copilot and will support GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry by the end of 2026. Google, Amazon and OpenAI are also building their own chips to rely less on Nvidia. Microsoft plans to share more at its Ignite conference next month.

3 things to know
  1. Maia 300 is Microsoft's third-gen chip for running AI models
  2. Microsoft claims 40% more tokens per megawatt than top GPU systems
  3. It becomes the main AI engine for Microsoft products from 2027
