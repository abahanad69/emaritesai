Google DeepMind launches an institute to widen the AGI debate

Shane Legg, James Manyika and Demis Hassabis direct the new body, which opens with four essays.

2026-09-19 · EmaritesAI

Google and Google DeepMind researchers launched the DeepMind Institute on Wednesday, a body meant to widen the conversation about artificial general intelligence rather than settle it.

DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg, Google executive James Manyika and Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis are listed as directors, with Legg as managing editor. The announcement is explicit that contributors will disagree with each other and change their minds as the frontier moves.

The opening collection has four essays. One, by safety researchers Rohin Shah and Anca Dragan, argues that AI's shrinking window of transparency — the ability to read a model's step-by-step reasoning — is a choice rather than an inevitability. They suggest limiting "opaque serial depth," the amount of sequential computation a model can do without producing a readable trace, or requiring developers to show that less transparent systems stay equally monitorable.

In another, Hassabis proposes a US-led standards body for frontier models. Developers would submit models voluntarily up to 30 days before release, and once the evaluation system proved itself, the review would carry more weight.

The other two essays cover economic policy for AGI-driven disruption and principles for human flourishing.

3 things to know
  1. Directors are Shane Legg, James Manyika and Demis Hassabis
  2. Essay urges limits on reasoning developers cannot make human-readable
  3. Hassabis proposes a US-led frontier model standards body
