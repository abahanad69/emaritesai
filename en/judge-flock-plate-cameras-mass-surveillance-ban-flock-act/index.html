US judge calls Flock plate-camera searches 'mass surveillance'

The ruling lands as US lawmakers introduce a bill to bar federal agencies from using license plate readers.

2026-10-04 · EmaritesAI

A US federal judge has ruled that a sheriff's deputy in Tulsa, Oklahoma, broke the Fourth Amendment when he searched Flock Safety's network for a driver's license plate without a warrant. Flock sells cameras that automatically read and log the plates of passing cars.

Judge Sara Hill wrote that the deputy had no clear reason for the search beyond the car's out-of-state plate. She ordered that all evidence found after the Flock search be suppressed. She also described the system as a form of 'indiscriminate mass surveillance', because it records every vehicle that passes a connected camera and hands the data to police on request. The ruling does not set binding precedent, but TechCrunch, citing 404 Media, reports it is among the first in which a federal judge found a Flock search unconstitutional.

On October 2, Senators Bernie Sanders and Jeff Merkley and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez introduced a bill that would bar federal agencies from using automated license plate readers. Reports say it would also pause federal grants to local governments that hold Flock contracts and let people sue the federal government over such use.

Flock runs more than 120,000 cameras across 49 states, according to Hoodline. Several states and cities have already dropped the technology. The company's chief legal officer said Americans' privacy should not depend on one vendor's judgment, and pointed to Flock's own policy changes.

3 things to know
  1. A federal judge ruled a warrantless Flock plate search unconstitutional.
  2. All evidence found after the search was ordered suppressed.
  3. A new bill would bar federal agencies from using plate-reading cameras.
