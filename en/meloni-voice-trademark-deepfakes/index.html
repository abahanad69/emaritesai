Italy's Meloni files to trademark her voice against AI deepfakes

The application to the EU trademark office uses a four-second clip of her saying 'Io sono Giorgia'.

2026-10-07 · EmaritesAI

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni has applied to register her voice as a trademark with the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO), the BBC reports. The filing was made on Monday and appears on the EUIPO website, and her office confirmed it.

The application includes a four-second audio recording in which Meloni says "Io sono Giorgia", meaning "I am Giorgia", twice. The phrase was the title of her 2021 autobiography and is tied to her political campaigns. Italy holds general elections next year.

The aim is to give her a legal tool against AI voice clones, which are made by training a model on recordings of a real person. A rough clone can be built from a few seconds of audio, while convincing copies usually need hours.

Meloni is not the first public figure to try this route. Actor Matthew McConaughey registered his image and voice in January, and Taylor Swift filed three US trademark applications in April, two of them audio clips. In the UK, more than 80 performers asked the government in August for a legal right for every person to own their voice.

Whether a voice trademark can stop deepfakes is untested. Trademarks mainly protect commercial use, so how far this filing reaches will depend on how EUIPO and the courts treat it.

  2. The filing is a 4-second clip of her saying 'Io sono Giorgia' twice
  3. McConaughey and Taylor Swift made similar filings earlier this year
