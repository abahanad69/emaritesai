Japan calls a cyber emergency after attacks hit 20+ big firms

Officials and analysts say AI is lowering the cost of attacks and helping hackers past the Japanese-language barrier.

2026-10-12 · EmaritesAI

Japan's cybersecurity chief has described the country as being in a state of emergency in cyberspace. Masaaki Taira, who heads the National Cybersecurity Strategy Headquarters, made the remark at an emergency meeting on Friday, October 9, the Financial Times reported.

The meeting followed a run of disclosures. More than 20 major Japanese companies have reported cyberattacks in recent weeks, among them the convenience store chain Lawson, the car rental firm Times Car and the rail operator JR East. Reports put the possible exposure at tens of millions of customer records. One leak included a large store of photographs of driver's licences.

Experts quoted by the FT and others partly attribute the surge to AI. Their argument is that AI tools make capable attacks cheaper and faster to run, and help foreign groups write convincing Japanese, which used to limit who could target Japanese firms and users.

The government has started to respond. The Financial Services Agency told lenders to accept only identity documents that carry an ID chip, and ministries are passing security guidance from the National Cybersecurity Office to local authorities and companies. The advice covers updates, strong passwords and tighter access controls.

More than 500 incidents have been reported in Japan so far in 2026, according to figures cited by PYMNTS, compared with 473 in all of 2025 and 503 in 2024.
