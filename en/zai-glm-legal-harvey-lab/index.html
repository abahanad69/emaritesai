Z.ai's GLM-Legal scores 19.2% on Harvey's legal agent test

The legal model nearly triples its base model's score, but it is open only to firms that apply for the beta.

2026-10-13 · EmaritesAI

Z.ai, the Chinese developer of the GLM models, introduced GLM-Legal on October 12. It is built on GLM-5.3-Flash, a mixture-of-experts model with 320 billion total and 18 billion active parameters, and trained further on legal texts and then with reinforcement learning across what Z.ai calls tens of thousands of legal task environments.

The headline result is on Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark, an open test of 1,250 tasks across 24 practice areas. A task counts only if every one of its expert-written criteria is met. Z.ai reports that GLM-Legal passes 19.2% of tasks, against about 6.7% for its base model. In Z.ai's chart, Kimi K3 scores 12.92%, Claude Fable 5 11.25% and GPT-6 Astra 5.42%, according to MarkTechPost. The same report says Vals AI ranks GLM-Legal seventh of 79 models, with Muse Spark 1.2 on top at 25.42%, and that six higher-ranked models were left out of Z.ai's chart.

The low numbers show how hard full legal work still is for AI: even the leader fails most tasks outright. On individual criteria, GLM-Legal passes 92.5%, MarkTechPost reports.

The model is not publicly available. Z.ai offers it through its hosted API to law firms, in-house teams and legal-tech companies that apply as design partners, with no published price and no open weights. The company says GLM-Legal will later come to its AutoClaw desktop agent, without giving a date.

3 things to know
  1. GLM-Legal passes 19.2% of Harvey LAB tasks, per Z.ai
  2. Its base model, GLM-5.3-Flash, scores about 6.7%
  3. Access is by application only, with no price or open weights
