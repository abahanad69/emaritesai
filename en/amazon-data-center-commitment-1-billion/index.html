Amazon pledges $1B to data center towns as local opposition grows

AWS chief Matt Garman says over 100 moratoriums are under consideration and drops government NDAs.

2026-10-03 · EmaritesAI

Amazon is trying to win back communities that are pushing against AI data centers. In a long post published on October 2, Amazon Web Services CEO Matt Garman set out a four-part Data Center Commitment and argued that the build-out is vital to US competitiveness and security.

Garman said more than 100 data center moratoriums are now being considered across the country. He warned that if they pass, the US could hand an advantage to rivals, and he blamed part of the opposition on misinformation, including what he called reports of foreign efforts to slow American construction.

The commitments are concrete. Amazon says it will put $1 billion over five years into a program called Built Together for communities near its sites, and work with utilities so that its facilities do not raise local electricity bills. It also says it no longer uses nondisclosure agreements with the government agencies it works with, a practice that had drawn questions from US lawmakers.

On the environment, Amazon repeated its goal to be water positive by 2030 and said it was 75% of the way there as of 2025. It also cited an average energy-efficiency rating, or PUE, of 1.14.

The post shows how public acceptance, not only chips and power, now shapes AI expansion. For Gulf states racing to host large AI campuses, the US debate is an early test of how these projects win local support.

3 things to know
  1. AWS promises $1B over five years for communities near data centers
  2. Garman says 100+ local moratoriums are under consideration in the US
  3. Amazon drops NDAs with government agencies and vows no bill increases
