Microsoft's Brad Smith says washing machines face more rules than AI

The Microsoft president calls for global AI safety rules, incident reports and kill switches, and says the UK is best placed to lead.

2026-10-12 · EmaritesAI

Microsoft president Brad Smith has called for binding global safety rules for AI, arguing that the technology is held to a lower standard than household goods. Speaking in the BBC's Big Boss Interview, published on October 11, he said "your washing machine is subject to more safety rules than AI", and pointed to the inspections that milk goes through before it reaches shops.

Smith set out four measures. Companies would have to write incident reports and notify victims if an AI system goes rogue. Systems would need emergency brakes, or kill switches, to slow or stop them. Independent government evaluators would test new models before release. And the rules should be agreed internationally rather than country by country.

He said the UK is best placed to lead, citing its AI Security Institute, and argued that Washington would accept UK-led standards more readily than rules from the European Union.

The comments put Smith at odds with the Trump administration's preference for self-regulation. At the end of September, President Trump hosted leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, Nvidia, Meta, Google and SpaceX, who signed what the BBC called a morally binding document committing them to work with independent auditors. Smith said Microsoft was happy to sign it but that voluntary pledges are no longer sufficient.

His remarks follow Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella's call last week for an emergency brake on advanced AI.

3 things to know
  1. Brad Smith: a washing machine faces more safety rules than AI
  2. He wants incident reports, kill switches and pre-release government tests
  3. Smith says voluntary White House pledges are no longer enough
