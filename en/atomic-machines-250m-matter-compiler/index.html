Atomic Machines raises $250M to build micro-machines from code

The startup from Amazon Prime's Jeff Holden starts with a fast power switch for AI data centres.

2026-10-11 · EmaritesAI

Atomic Machines, a Bay Area startup founded by Jeff Holden, has disclosed $250 million in funding after six years in stealth, The Next Web reported. Holden joined Amazon as its tenth engineer and helped build Amazon Prime, then became Uber's first chief product officer.

Investors include OneIM, Gigafund, XTX Ventures, KAS Venture Partners, Valor Equity Partners, Sozo Ventures and the Regents of the University of California. No valuation was disclosed.

The company's system, called the Matter Compiler, fabricates devices directly from code. According to the company, it can build machines with moving parts and multiple materials, with features down to single-digit microns, and needs no moulds, masks or fixed tooling. It measures and corrects each step as it works.

The first product is aimed at AI infrastructure. The PrimeSwitch PS-150 is a relay that opens in 50 microseconds, which the company says is about 1,000 times faster than a conventional contactor. It carries 150 amps of continuous current and measures 9.5 by 3 millimetres. It targets data centres moving to 800-volt direct current power, where fast switching helps protect equipment.

The switch is shipping to unnamed early-access customers for evaluation. Atomic Machines plans its public debut at the Open Compute Project Global Summit in San Jose from October 12 to 15. Longer term it describes goals such as tiny gears, robots small enough to work inside the body, and cooling built into AI chips.
