OpenAI disrupts Russian and Iranian fake-front influence campaigns

One used a fake research centre to target Latin America; the other invented seven journalists who wrote almost 100 articles.

2026-10-09 · EmaritesAI

OpenAI says it has disrupted two influence operations that used its models while hiding behind fake organisations and personas. One is linked to Russia and the other to Iran. OpenAI calls these false-front operations, because they invent a front such as a research group or a reporter to hide who is really behind the message.

The Russian operation, which OpenAI named Dark Clark, targeted audiences in Latin America. Its goals were to undermine support for Ukraine and to influence politics in Argentina and Bolivia. It ran through a fake organisation called the Social Research Center, presented by a persona named Mia Clark, and posted on X, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok.

OpenAI rated Dark Clark Category 5 on the Breakout Scale, a six-level measure of how far an operation's content spreads beyond its own fake accounts. The company says it is the first Category 5 operation it has disrupted.

The Iranian operation, named Bogus Bylines, created seven fake journalist personas. Together they published almost 100 articles across about a dozen outlets. OpenAI rated the network's social media comments Category 2 and its articles Category 4, meaning the articles travelled much further than the comments.

OpenAI says it banned the accounts involved and shared information with the relevant authorities.
