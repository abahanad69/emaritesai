What is an AI agent, and how is it different from a chatbot?

OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse are agents: they take a goal and act on it, not just answer.

2026-09-30 · EmaritesAI

This week OpenAI launched Dots, weeks after Meta's Muse took off. Both are AI agents, a step beyond the chatbots most people know.

A chatbot works turn by turn: you ask, it answers, and nothing happens until you ask again. An agent is given a goal instead, such as "sell this chair on Marketplace" or "fix the bugs customers report", and works out the steps itself.

To carry those steps out, an agent uses tools: a web browser, apps, code, email or messaging. Dots, for example, run on their own cloud computer with access to thousands of apps.

Agents work in a loop. They act, look at the result, adjust the plan and try again, often for a long time without a person watching. Many also keep memory of the task and learn your preferences.

That autonomy is the point, and also the risk. An agent can only touch what it is allowed to, so its permissions decide what can go wrong. This week a YouTuber said Muse shared his home address with a Marketplace buyer after he gave it broad permission. Knowing what an agent can reach is now part of using AI safely.

Explained simply
  1. A goal, not a question: You hand it an outcome and it plans the steps itself.
  2. Tools: It acts through a browser, apps, code or email, not just text.
  3. The loop: Act, check the result, adjust, repeat, often with no one watching.
  4. Memory: It keeps track of the task and can learn your preferences.
  5. Permissions: What it's allowed to touch decides what can go wrong.
3 things to know
  1. A chatbot answers; an agent takes a goal and acts
  2. Agents use tools: browser, apps, code, email
  3. Permissions decide what an agent can get wrong
Sources: The Verge, The Verge
