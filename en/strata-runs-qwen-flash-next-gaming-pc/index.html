Strata runs Qwen's giant model on a 12GB gaming GPU

The free open source installer puts Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on a home PC, with developer-measured speeds of up to 94 tokens a second.

2026-10-05 · EmaritesAI

A free open source project called Strata is drawing attention for running Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a large model that normally needs a server, on a home gaming PC. It reached the top of Hacker News over the weekend with more than 600 points.

The project describes the model as having 125 billion parameters. It is a mixture-of-experts design with 24,576 small expert modules, of which only 10 are used for each word. Strata keeps the busiest parts on the graphics card and the rest in system memory, and uses compressed versions of the model at 2 to 4 bits.

The requirements are a graphics card with at least 12GB of memory, from NVIDIA's RTX 20 to 50 series or AMD's RX 7000 and 9000 series, 32GB of RAM and about 80GB of disk space. It runs on Windows and Linux.

According to the developers' own tests, an RTX 5070 with 12GB wrote answers at up to 94 tokens a second and read prompts at 2,650 tokens a second. An AMD RX 9070 XT reached 60 tokens a second for writing.

Strata exposes a local endpoint compatible with OpenAI's and Anthropic's APIs, so coding agents and chat apps can connect to it, and the project says nothing leaves the PC. It is released under the MIT license and is not affiliated with Alibaba's Qwen team.

The first start can freeze the PC for a few minutes while it loads 35 to 55GB into memory.

3 things to know
  1. Needs a 12GB GPU, 32GB of RAM and about 80GB of disk
  2. Developers measured up to 94 tokens per second on an RTX 5070
  3. Free under the MIT license, with OpenAI- and Anthropic-style local APIs
