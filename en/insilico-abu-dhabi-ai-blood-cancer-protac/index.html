Insilico's Abu Dhabi team uses AI to design blood cancer drug leads

The compounds are designed to destroy BTK, a protein that drives some leukaemias and lymphomas, and are still at the pre-clinical stage.

2026-10-05 · EmaritesAI

Scientists at Insilico Medicine's research centre in Masdar City, Abu Dhabi, have used AI to design candidate drugs for blood cancers, The National reported on Sunday.

The compounds are PROTACs: molecules that tag a target protein so the cell's own disposal system destroys it, rather than only blocking it. Their target is Bruton's tyrosine kinase, or BTK, a protein that helps malignant B cells grow in diseases such as chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and mantle cell lymphoma.

The team used two of the company's AI platforms: PandaOmics to identify disease-related targets, and Chemistry42 to design molecules, including two new binding parts that recruit the cell's disposal machinery.

According to the paper in the Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, published online in August, the two lead compounds, ISM-PR25 and ISM-PR44, broke down BTK in malignant B cells at very low concentrations, with half-maximal values of 0.04 and 0.05 nanomolar. In mice, 26% and 29% of an oral dose reached the bloodstream.

Insilico says AI can cut the time to select a drug candidate to 12 to 18 months, compared with 2.5 to 4 years using traditional methods. The compounds are still lead candidates and need further tests before they can be considered for clinical trials.

Earlier this year, the same Abu Dhabi team announced ISM0387, which the company describes as the first drug candidate discovered in the UAE.

3 things to know
  1. AI-designed PROTACs target BTK, a driver of some blood cancers
  2. Lead compounds broke down BTK at 0.04-0.05 nanomolar in cells
  3. Still pre-clinical: more tests are needed before any human trial
