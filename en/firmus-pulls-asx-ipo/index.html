Nvidia-backed Firmus pulls its Australian IPO amid valuation doubts

The AI data centre builder cited market volatility after big investors balked at a roughly A$44 billion price tag.

2026-10-09 · EmaritesAI

Firmus, an Australian builder of AI data centres backed by Nvidia, has abandoned plans for what would have been one of the country's biggest stock market listings. The company said recent market volatility meant going public was not in the interest of the company or its shareholders, and that it will now seek capital from private markets.

The listing would have valued Firmus at about A$44 billion, according to ABC News, or more than US$30 billion, as the BBC put it. ABC reports the company had aimed to raise around A$7 billion.

Several large investors stayed out. UniSuper, one of Australia's biggest pension funds, said Firmus had a compelling story but not a compelling valuation, and raised concerns about the debt it would need to grow. ABC reports Firmus expects about US$30 billion in debt once its data centres are built.

Firmus builds liquid-cooled data centres it calls AI factories, with clients including OpenAI and Meta, and operations in Australia, Singapore and elsewhere in Asia-Pacific. Its backers include Nvidia, which holds a 7.2% stake according to ABC, as well as Blackstone and Jane Street.

The decision comes as investors question the hundreds of billions being spent on AI infrastructure. Nvidia and Oracle shares fell on Thursday after reports that OpenAI's revenue was lower than earlier figures suggested.
