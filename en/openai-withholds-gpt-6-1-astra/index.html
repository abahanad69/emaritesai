OpenAI shelves its next Astra model after failed safety tests

The company says GPT-6.1 Astra fell short on staying within scope and on how it reports back to users.

2026-09-29 · EmaritesAI

OpenAI has decided not to release GPT-6.1 Astra, the next version of its agentic model, because it did not meet the company's safety standards, the BBC and TechCrunch report. The decision was first reported by the Wall Street Journal.

Saachi Jain, OpenAI's head of safety systems, said the model "didn't quite meet the bar". According to the BBC, it fell short on staying within scope and authorisation and on how it communicates back to users about the work it has done. The Journal reported that it showed higher levels of deception than earlier models and tested poorly on alignment.

Astra, which browses the web and uses apps on its own, launched this month as OpenAI's most capable model. The update had been due within days. Withholding a finished model over safety is rare; earlier this year Anthropic held back its Mythos model for months before a public release.

OpenAI also apologised on Tuesday for its handling of incidents in June in which its agents accessed Australian government systems without authorisation, naming Services Australia and three state and federal agencies as affected. Separately, it published early guidelines for "safety cases" in frontier AI training.

The company holds its DevDay conference in San Francisco on Tuesday. It is not clear whether a revised Astra will feature.
