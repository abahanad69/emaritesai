AlphaChip's creators want AI to design a chip in about a week

Founders Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini told TechCrunch Disrupt that faster chip design could ease the compute shortage.

2026-10-15 · EmaritesAI

Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini, who previously built AlphaChip, an AI system for laying out chips, are now trying to automate much more of chip design with their startup, Ricursive Intelligence. They described the plan on stage at TechCrunch Disrupt in San Francisco on Wednesday.

Goldie said the industry expects at least 10,000 times more demand for compute over the next five years, and called that estimate possibly conservative. Building 10,000 times more data centres is not the answer, she argued. Instead, Ricursive wants chips that make better use of data centre bandwidth, silicon wafers and fabrication plants.

The company's AI designs chips, learns from each round and uses what it learned on the next design. The founders said they optimise entire chips at once rather than splitting them into dozens of smaller blocks, as traditional design does. They pointed to AlphaChip, which produced a circular, donut-shaped arrangement of components that surprised engineers but worked, and said their new approach creates wave-like patterns they will share more about later.

Speed is the main pitch. A new chip can take six months to two years to design, Goldie said, which forces companies to be very selective. If design took about a week, she said, it could make financial sense to build custom chips for space, AR and VR or biological computing, and to make them more energy efficient. Goldie said humans would bring the ideas while AI handles execution. Ricursive did not share customers, funding figures or a product timeline on stage.

3 things to know
  1. Ricursive is founded by the researchers behind Google's AlphaChip
  2. Goldie expects at least 10,000x more compute demand in five years
  3. The goal: cut chip design from up to 2 years to about a week
Sources: TechCrunch
⚠️ Goldie and Mirhoseini founded Ricursive and previously developed AlphaChip
