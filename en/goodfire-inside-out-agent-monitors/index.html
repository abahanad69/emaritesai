Goodfire's probes watch AI agents from the inside, for far less

The startup says its monitors caught 94% of malicious hacking sessions for about $51, against $10,000 for a top AI checker.

2026-10-09 · EmaritesAI

Goodfire, an AI interpretability startup, has launched what it calls inside-out monitors for AI agents, TechCrunch reports. They are available to customers of Baseten, a company that hosts and runs AI models for other businesses.

The usual way to watch an agent is to have a second AI model read every step it takes. That works, but it is expensive. Goodfire instead places small detectors, called probes, on the model's internal signals as it works. Only when a probe flags something does a separate AI model take a closer look. 'Internal activation monitors are really cheap because they reuse the computations in the forward pass,' chief executive Eric Ho said.

In Goodfire's tests on the open model Kimi K3 across about 1,500 sessions, the probes cost about $51 to run. A cheaper AI model checking every step cost about $233, and a top-tier model about $10,000. The probes caught 94% of malicious hacking sessions, while sending 8.7% of harmless sessions for a second look. Running four probes at once added less than 2% to response time, the company says.

Customers choose which risks to watch, including offensive hacking and reward hacking, and whether to log, escalate to a human or refuse. All results are Goodfire's own and have not been independently tested.
