Anthropic commits $100M to train 10,000 enterprise AI engineers

The new Claude Frontier Academy targets the shortage of people who can put AI into production.

2026-10-03 · EmaritesAI

Anthropic has launched Claude Frontier Academy, a training program backed by a $100 million commitment. The company says it wants to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers, specialists who take AI from pilot to production inside a business, by the end of 2027.

The pitch targets a real bottleneck. Many companies have access to capable models but lack staff who can turn them into working systems. Anthropic argues that a small group of skilled engineers often delivers most of the value, and it wants to build that talent inside its customers and partners.

The first program, called the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency, is modelled on medical training. Nominated engineers attend a multi-day, in-person course with Anthropic staff, work through a simulated enterprise deployment and sit a graded practical. Those who pass earn a Claude Resident Engineer badge, then spend 12 weeks leading a real Claude project at their own employer before a final assessment.

Inaugural cohorts include engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk. Sessions are running in San Francisco, New York and London, and Anthropic expects the first full Frontier Deployed Engineer badges in early 2027.

Entry is by nomination only, through an organisation's Anthropic account team. The company has not said whether participants pay or announced any cohorts in the Gulf.
