Pentagon says it has stopped using Anthropic's Claude

A defence official told the BBC the department has ended all use of Anthropic products, weeks after its own deadline passed.

2026-10-06 · EmaritesAI

The US Department of Defense has stopped using Anthropic's AI tools, a department official told the BBC on Monday. The statement said the Pentagon "has ceased the use of Anthropic products".

Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a national security supply-chain risk in February and set a six-month deadline, from 27 February, to phase it out. That deadline passed in late August. Several people familiar with the department's work told the BBC that Claude was still being used for research, analysis and intelligence work as recently as last week.

According to those sources, Claude was built into the Maven Smart System, the Palantir-run platform the Pentagon uses to organise intelligence data. A Georgetown analyst told the BBC that deep integration of this kind makes a model hard to remove quickly.

The dispute began when the Pentagon asked Anthropic to drop safety limits on Claude. Anthropic refused, citing concerns about mass surveillance and autonomous weapons, and has sued to overturn the designation, which it calls unlawful. The Pentagon has since signed contracts with Google, xAI and OpenAI.

Anthropic declined to comment on the statement. The company's relations with the White House appear to be improving: President Trump met Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei twice last week. The court case over the designation is still under way.
