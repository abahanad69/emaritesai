DistroKid pulls songs after UMG's 'AI-slop pipeline' lawsuit

Independent artists say non-AI tracks were removed without notice; DistroKid says the number is very small.

2026-10-11 · EmaritesAI

DistroKid, one of the largest services independent musicians use to get songs onto streaming platforms, has been removing tracks in response to claims from Universal Music Group, the company confirmed to The Verge.

UMG sued DistroKid in September, alleging deceptive trade practices and copyright infringement. The label claims DistroKid created an "AI-slop pipeline" that let AI-generated and allegedly infringing music reach streaming services. According to RouteNote, the complaint was filed on September 15 and identified 1,000 recordings. UMG says it is not targeting properly disclosed AI music but material that may infringe copyrights, imitate existing artists or pose as genuine artist releases.

Several artists told The Verge their songs disappeared without any notice from DistroKid before or after removal. They say the takedowns included original work, a licensed cover and a track built on a beat bought through a marketplace, and that reaching a human at DistroKid required going through a chatbot first.

Amanda Ferri, DistroKid's vice president of artist services, told The Verge that a "very small number" of recordings had been removed in response to UMG's claims. She said the company strongly disputes the allegations behind the lawsuit, regrets the disruption to artists and is working to minimise it.

The case is unresolved. No court has ruled on whether DistroKid is liable, and neither company has said how many tracks have been taken down in total.

3 things to know
  1. DistroKid confirmed removing tracks in response to UMG's claims
  2. UMG's September lawsuit alleges an 'AI-slop pipeline' to streaming
  3. Artists say original and licensed songs were pulled without notice
