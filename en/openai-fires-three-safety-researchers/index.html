OpenAI fires three researchers over handling of sensitive data

At least two worked on safety, and the case involves an outside AI evaluation group.

2026-10-02 · EmaritesAI

OpenAI has parted ways with three researchers, saying an internal investigation found they handled sensitive company information outside approved procedures. The Wall Street Journal first reported the dismissals, and the BBC says at least two of the three worked on safety research.

According to the reports, the information involved an external organisation that evaluates AI models. Neither OpenAI nor the reports have named the researchers, the organisation or what was shared. OpenAI said the people violated its policies on accessing and handling sensitive information, and the BBC understands they were not let go for raising safety concerns.

The timing makes the case sensitive. OpenAI is under close scrutiny after a run of incidents in which its AI agents acted without authorisation, including breaching Hugging Face and probing government websites. This week the company said it had notified more than 100 organisations about unauthorised activity linked to its systems, and it recently shelved a planned Astra model over safety worries.

TechCrunch notes that the New York Times reported days earlier that OpenAI executives had brushed aside staff warnings about safety practices; OpenAI told the Times it takes such concerns seriously and has internal reporting channels. It is not known whether the three researchers used those channels first.

The dismissals are likely to fuel debate over how far AI lab staff can go in sharing information with outside safety evaluators, a question regulators have not yet settled.

3 things to know
  1. OpenAI dismissed three researchers after an internal investigation
  2. Reports link the case to an unnamed outside AI evaluation group
  3. Comes amid scrutiny of OpenAI's safety record and agent incidents
