Google Play now installs your private AI-built apps on Android

Apps made in Google AI Studio can now run on your own phone through Play without being listed in the public store.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

Google Play can now install apps that people build for themselves with AI. Google announced on October 13 that an Android app created by describing it in Google AI Studio can be launched straight to the creator's phone through Play, using an ordinary Google account.

Until now, getting such an app onto an Android phone meant connecting the phone to a computer or publishing to the public Play Store, which reaches about 3 billion users, according to TechCrunch. The new personal apps stay out of Play's search results and listings and are visible only to the person who made them. They still have to follow Google Play's policies.

The feature starts in the US for users aged 18 and over. Google first let people vibe-code personal Android apps in AI Studio in May. In the coming months it plans to let creators share an app with a small group of trusted people, and to offer a path to full publication after the same Trust and Safety review that professional developers go through.

Sam Bright, who runs Google Play, framed the change as a shift toward apps made for a single person. The same announcement included Game Finder, an AI game recommendation tool in the US, comparison answers from the Ask Play assistant, and an age-range signal that lets parents share a child's age band with developers instead of a birth date.

3 things to know
  1. Apps built in Google AI Studio can install privately through Google Play
  2. Personal apps are hidden from Play search and seen only by their maker
  3. US only, ages 18+; sharing with a small group comes later
