How do AI benchmarks work, and what can't they tell you?

A benchmark is a fixed test with a score, and who ran it matters as much as the number.

2026-10-08 · EmaritesAI

A benchmark is a fixed set of questions or tasks that every model attempts in the same way, plus a rule for turning the answers into a score. Because the test stays the same, scores from different models can be compared.

The metric tells you which direction is good. This week TII reported that its Falcon-ASR speech model has a 20.92% word error rate across six Arabic test sets, so lower is better. Google's model card for Nano Banana 2.1 uses Elo ratings from human preference votes, where higher is better: 1,050 against 990 for the previous model.

Three questions help. Who ran the test? Launch numbers are usually reported by the company itself, and both examples above are vendor results. Was the setup the same? Settings and prompts can shift a score. Could the model have seen the test? If test questions leak into training data, a high score can reflect memory rather than skill.

Benchmarks are useful for spotting big differences. Small gaps often matter less than how a model handles the tasks you actually do.

Explained simply
  1. A fixed test: Every model faces the same questions or tasks, so the scores can be compared.
  2. Read the metric: Check whether higher or lower is better, as with Elo versus word error rate.
  3. Who ran it: Launch scores usually come from the vendor; independent leaderboards rerun tests.
  4. Watch for leaks: If test questions reach training data, a high score may reflect memory.
  5. Test it yourself: Try a model on a few of your real tasks before trusting any ranking.
3 things to know
  1. A benchmark is a fixed test plus a rule that turns answers into a score
  2. Check the metric: for error rates lower is better, for Elo higher
  3. Launch scores are usually vendor-reported; test on your own tasks
