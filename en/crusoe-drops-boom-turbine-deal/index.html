Crusoe drops $1.25B deal for Boom Supersonic's turbines

The AI data-centre builder was the launch customer for Boom's 42 MW Superpower gas turbines.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Crusoe, one of the largest builders of AI data centres, has ended plans to use stationary power plants from Boom Supersonic, TechCrunch reports.

Crusoe had signed on as the first customer for Boom's new power business, agreeing to spend $1.25 billion on 29 of its 42-megawatt Superpower turbines, with deliveries due to start in 2027. The natural-gas turbines share about 80% of their parts with Symphony, the engine Boom is developing for its Overture supersonic jet.

Boom CEO Blake Scholl said on X that the companies are no longer moving forward with the partnership, adding that Boom will deliver about 250 MW of Superpowers to other sites next year and is targeting 1 GW in 2028. A Crusoe spokesperson said its energy plans "haven't changed" and that it still plans to use turbines, just not Boom's, alongside wind, solar, batteries and the grid.

Crusoe's 1.2-gigawatt Abilene, Texas campus for Oracle and OpenAI runs on grid power with gas turbines for backup, while a 900 MW site it is building there for Microsoft will use on-site gas turbines. For Boom, which raised $300 million last year largely to commercialise the power business and help fund Overture, losing its launch customer is a setback.

3 things to know

  2. Crusoe still plans turbines, just not Boom's
  3. Boom targets ~250 MW of deliveries next year, 1 GW in 2028
