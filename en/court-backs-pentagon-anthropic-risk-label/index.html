Appeals court lets Pentagon keep Anthropic supply-chain risk label

The DC Circuit declined to overturn one of two designations; Anthropic says it is weighing all options.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Anthropic has lost a legal bid to overturn one of the supply-chain risk labels the US Department of Defense applied to the company, Wired reports. A federal appeals court in Washington, DC declined on Friday to second-guess the administration.

"The department had ample support for its conclusion," the judges wrote in the majority opinion, finding that continued integration of Claude into defence information systems presented a statutorily covered national-security risk. The opinion pointed to Anthropic's own acknowledgement that it builds restrictions into Claude that stop the model from doing tasks the company wants to prevent.

Earlier this year the Pentagon sanctioned Anthropic under two separate supply-chain laws, aiming to remove Claude from the military and other parts of the federal government by this month. The dispute began after Anthropic executives said the company would not allow its current models to support autonomous weapons or domestic surveillance. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth described that stance as a significant national-security risk.

Anthropic spokesperson Danielle Cohen said the company remains confident in its position and is considering all options. That could mean asking the full DC Circuit to rehear the case or taking it to the US Supreme Court.

The ruling covers one of the two designations; the other has to be challenged separately.

3 things to know
  1. DC appeals court upheld one of two Pentagon risk designations
  2. Dispute began over Anthropic's limits on weapons and surveillance use
  3. Anthropic is weighing further appeals, possibly to the Supreme Court
