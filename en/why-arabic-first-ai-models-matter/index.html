Why do AI models need to be built for Arabic first?

Dialects, mixed-language speech and local test sets are where general models slip.

2026-10-10 · EmaritesAI

Arabic is one written standard and many spoken dialects. A model that reads news articles well can still miss the meaning of a voice note from Dubai or Casablanca.

The reason is data. TII, the Abu Dhabi lab behind Falcon, says most language models learn Arabic mainly from Modern Standard Arabic, so they can miss meaning in dialect, poetry and proverbs. Its Falcon-Emirati model, released this week, has 7 billion parameters and was trained on native Emirati content and material about Emirati culture.

Speech adds another layer. Many Arabic speakers switch to English or French in the middle of a sentence. Mistral's new Voxtral Arabic model, released on October 8, was built for that kind of code-switching as well as for dialects.

Testing matters too. A model can score well on a benchmark written in formal Arabic and still fail in a call centre. That is why newer releases come with their own dialect test sets, such as TII's Alyah benchmark for Emirati and the Darija in the Wild set for Moroccan Arabic.

When you choose a tool for Arabic work, ask which dialects it was tested on, not only whether it supports Arabic.

Explained simply
  1. Dialects differ: A model fluent in formal Arabic can still miss meaning in Emirati or Darija.
  2. Data decides: Arabic-first models train on native dialect content, not only formal text.
  3. Speech mixes languages: Good Arabic speech models handle switching to English or French.
  4. Test locally: Look for dialect benchmarks such as Alyah or Darija in the Wild.
3 things to know
  1. Most models learn Arabic mainly from formal Modern Standard Arabic
  2. Arabic-first models train on dialects and mixed-language speech
  3. Ask which dialects a tool was tested on before you rely on it
