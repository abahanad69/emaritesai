TypeSafe raises $870M at $7.5B weeks after launching Jev

Andreessen Horowitz led the round for the maker of an AI model that returns decisions instead of text.

2026-10-10 · EmaritesAI

TypeSafe AI has raised $870 million at a $7.5 billion valuation, less than a month after it released its first model, Jev, on September 15. Andreessen Horowitz led the round, with Sequoia Capital and existing investor DCVC taking part, and a16z partner Martin Casado is joining the board, according to the company.

Jev is built on the transformer architecture used by chatbots, but it is not a language model. It does not write text. It returns probabilities, which TypeSafe calls calibrated decisions, and the company pitches it for automating software tasks rather than generating prose or code. TypeSafe says Jev runs faster and uses far fewer tokens than large language models. Those performance claims come from the company and have not been independently tested in public.

Adoption has been quick by the company's account. TypeSafe says a third of Fortune 500 companies are already using Jev and that it has saved customers millions of dollars in production. It plans to release more models of this type and add the enterprise features customers have asked for.

TypeSafe was founded in 2024 by Diogo Almeida, a former OpenAI researcher, Sasha Sheng, a former Meta research engineer, and engineer Erik Gafni. The raise follows Liquid AI's release this week of open models that also make decisions without writing text, so Jev is no longer the only model of its kind.
