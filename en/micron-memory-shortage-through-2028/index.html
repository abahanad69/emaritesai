Micron warns AI-driven memory shortage will last through 2028

Over 75% of Micron's 2027 memory output is already spoken for, its CEO says.

2026-10-02 · EmaritesAI

The memory crunch caused by the AI boom is not going away soon. On Micron's quarterly earnings call this week, chief executive Sanjay Mehrotra told investors the supply-demand balance is only getting tighter and that demand should exceed the company's supply through 2028. Ars Technica reports that Samsung executives voiced a similar outlook.

The pressure comes from AI data centres. Micron's sales are now focused on high-bandwidth memory (HBM), which sits beside AI accelerators, and on server DRAM. Mehrotra said demand for HBM is now outpacing demand for Micron's regular DRAM, and that more than 75% of the company's 2027 output is already accounted for. Most of its current sales talks are about 2028.

New capacity will take time. Micron plans to open new clean rooms in 2028, but Mehrotra cautioned that output in new facilities ramps up only gradually. Moving to newer HBM generations also eats into supply, because they need more wafer capacity for each bit, and future manufacturing node changes bring smaller gains than in the past.

Consumers will feel it too. Micron no longer sells memory directly to consumers, but when factories prioritise AI and servers, less capacity is left for the RAM in PCs and phones, which keeps prices high.

For AI companies, memory is becoming as strategic as GPUs. Expect more long-term supply deals and more pressure on device prices into 2027.

3 things to know
  1. Micron expects memory demand to exceed its supply through 2028
  2. More than 75% of Micron's 2027 output is already committed
  3. AI's appetite for HBM squeezes RAM supply for PCs and phones
