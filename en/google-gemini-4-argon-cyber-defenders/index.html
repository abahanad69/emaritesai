Google unveils Gemini 4 Argon, starting with cyber defenders

Google's first Gemini 4 model goes to vetted security partners before a wider launch.

2026-10-02 · EmaritesAI

Google has introduced Gemini 4 Argon, the first model in its Gemini 4 generation, and is handing it first to cybersecurity teams rather than the public. The company says Argon is being rolled out to trusted defenders through its Fairwind Program and will reach developers, enterprises and consumers once it has gathered feedback and tightened safeguards.

The staged release matters because Google is positioning Argon as a defensive security tool. According to Google, the model can autonomously find, validate and patch serious software vulnerabilities, a capability that could help defenders but would also be risky in the wrong hands.

Beyond security, Google pitches Argon for software engineering, long multi-step tasks and video understanding, and says its own engineers already use it for debugging and code migrations. Google lists a 1-million-token context window and introductory API pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising later to $4 and $20.

On benchmarks, Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 91.7% on the LVBench video test, and says Argon currently leads the Vals Index. These figures are reported by Google and have not yet been independently reproduced. Google also says the model went through internal and external red-team testing.

The launch lands days after OpenAI shelved a planned Astra model over safety concerns, sharpening the question of how labs release models with strong cyber skills. No date has been given for general availability.

3 things to know
  1. Gemini 4 Argon goes first to security partners in Google's Fairwind Program
  2. Google says it can find, validate and patch serious software flaws on its own
  3. Wider release to developers and consumers planned, with no date yet
