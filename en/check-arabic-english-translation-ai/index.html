Check an Arabic-English translation with AI in 4 steps

Use a chatbot as a second reader for meaning, tone and terms, then let a person sign off.

2026-10-07 · EmaritesAI

If you write in both Arabic and English, an AI chatbot can act as a fast second reader for translations. The trick is to ask it to check, not to rewrite.

Paste the source and the translation together, and say who the text is for: a government form, a customer email or a social post. Then ask the model to list every place where meaning was added, dropped or changed, with the line from each version side by side. A list is easier to verify than a rewritten paragraph.

Next, ask about register. Arabic switches between Standard Arabic and dialect, and a model may default to Standard Arabic even when your audience expects Gulf or Emirati phrasing. TII's work on Falcon-Emirati, released this week, found that many models answer in Standard Arabic when spoken to in dialect.

Then check names, numbers and dates, which are easy to slip during translation, and give the model your glossary of approved terms.

Treat the output as suggestions. For legal, medical or official documents, a qualified human translator should approve the final text.

Step by step
  1. Give context: paste both texts and say who will read them.
  2. Ask for a diff: list each added, dropped or changed meaning side by side.
  3. Check the register: say if you want Standard Arabic or a Gulf dialect.
  4. Verify the facts: names, numbers, dates and your approved glossary terms.
  5. Human sign-off: a qualified translator approves legal or official texts.
3 things to know
  1. Ask the AI to list differences, not to rewrite the translation
  2. Name the audience and the register you want: Standard or dialect
  3. A qualified translator should approve legal and official texts
Sources: TII on Hugging Face blog: Falcon-Emirati
⚠️ Many models answer in Standard Arabic when prompted in Emirati
