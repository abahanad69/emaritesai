Why do new AI models like Gemini 4 Argon think before answering?

Reasoning models write hidden working steps before the final reply, which helps on hard tasks and changes what you pay.

2026-10-14 · EmaritesAI

A reasoning model does not jump straight to an answer. It first generates a chain of internal steps, often called reasoning or thinking tokens, and only then writes the reply you see. OpenAI's developer guide says this helps with planning, tool use and tasks that need several steps.

Two models released this week work this way. Liquid AI says LFM2.5-24B-A2B always thinks before it answers, and Google describes Gemini 4 Argon as built for multi-step work in coding, law and science.

The extra thinking has costs. OpenAI's documentation says reasoning tokens are billed as output tokens and take up space in the context window, even though the raw reasoning is hidden and the API only returns an optional summary. A short question can therefore produce a much larger bill than the visible answer suggests.

Most providers let you control the effort. OpenAI exposes settings from low to high, and Google's Gemini API offers a thinking budget. Low effort suits quick lookups and rewrites. Higher effort pays off on maths, debugging and planning, where checking intermediate steps matters.

Explained simply
  1. Think, then answer: The model writes internal steps first, then the reply you see.
  2. Hidden but billed: Reasoning tokens count as output tokens even when you cannot read them.
  3. They use the window: Long reasoning takes context space and can cut off a reply.
  4. Set the effort: Use low effort for quick edits and high effort for maths or debugging.
3 things to know
  1. Reasoning models write hidden working steps before the visible answer
  2. Those reasoning tokens are billed as output and fill the context window
  3. Lower the effort setting for simple tasks to save time and money
