Anthropic's founders seek 50.1% voting control ahead of IPO

The seven co-founders would share special shares while the Long-Term Benefit Trust still picks most of the board.

2026-09-28 · EmaritesAI

Anthropic's founders want to stay in charge once the company is public. According to The Information, cited by TechCrunch, Anthropic is asking shareholders to approve a structure that would give CEO Dario Amodei and his six co-founders special shares carrying a combined 50.1% of the vote on most corporate matters, as long as at least three of them keep a minimum stake.

Super-voting shares are familiar — they are how Mark Zuckerberg kept control of Meta. What stands out here is that control would sit with a group rather than one founder. The seven co-founders reportedly own about 2% of the company each and have pledged to give away 80% of their wealth. The new shares would carry no extra economic value.

The plan comes with other changes. Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust would still choose most of the board; the founders' board seats would rise from two to three; and employees would receive their own class of stock to break ties on some issues.

The stakes are large. Anthropic was valued at $965 billion in May and has recently been valued at $1.5 trillion on the secondary market, a figure its expected IPO is likely to reflect. Shareholders are expected to vote in the coming days.

3 things to know
  1. Seven co-founders would jointly hold 50.1% of the vote
  2. Special shares carry no extra economic value
  3. Long-Term Benefit Trust would still pick most of the board
