Manus seeks a $4B valuation in a $500M round after Meta split

The AI agent startup is independent again after Beijing blocked its $2 billion sale to Meta.

2026-09-19 · EmaritesAI

Manus, the Chinese startup that went viral on the strength of an AI agent demo, is in discussions to raise $500 million at a $4 billion valuation, The Wall Street Journal reported, citing anonymous sources.

Potential investors named in the report include IDG Capital, Boyu Capital and battery maker Contemporary Amperex Technology, alongside existing backers Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund. The company is also said to be considering a restructuring to prepare for an initial public offering in Hong Kong.

The raise follows an unusually turbulent year. Manus moved its staff to Singapore in mid-2025 and announced a $2 billion acquisition by Meta that December, at a point when it was reported to be earning more than $100 million in annual recurring revenue. Beijing blocked the deal, citing possible violations of export controls and foreign investment rules amid wider concern about losing AI talent to the West.

Untangling took months. Early investors reportedly helped buy back shares at a valuation of about $2 billion, and in August the company told users to export their own data because it had to delete material generated after the Meta acquisition to meet regulatory requirements. Manus said this month that its founding team continues to lead it.

3 things to know
  1. Talks to raise $500M at a $4 billion valuation
  2. Beijing blocked Meta's $2B acquisition on export-control grounds
  3. A Hong Kong IPO is reportedly being prepared
