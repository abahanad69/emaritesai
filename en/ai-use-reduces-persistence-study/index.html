10 minutes of ChatGPT help cuts persistence on hard tasks, study finds

A Berkeley-led team found people did worse and gave up more once the AI was taken away.

2026-10-11 · EmaritesAI

Relying on an AI assistant for about 10 minutes can make people quicker to give up on hard problems once the assistant is gone, according to a study led by researchers at UC Berkeley.

The paper, "AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance," was presented at the Conference on Language Modeling (COLM) this week, Berkeley News reported on October 9. The team ran three randomized experiments with 1,222 participants recruited online. Two used basic fraction problems, with 354 and 667 people, and the third used SAT reading comprehension questions with 201 people.

In the fraction tests, participants worked through 15 problems. One group could ask ChatGPT for help, and the tool was removed after problem 12. That group started out more accurate, but its accuracy dropped almost immediately after removal and it skipped more problems. The group that never had AI stayed steady. In the reading test, both persistence and accuracy fell when AI was withdrawn.

Brian Christian of Berkeley's Center for Human-Compatible AI worked on the study with researchers from Carnegie Mellon, MIT, Oxford and UCLA. The authors suggest quick answers make slower, effortful work feel inefficient and remove the productive struggle that builds skill.

They argue AI tools should default to acting more like a tutor that guides people, rather than handing over answers. Several chatbots already offer optional learning modes, but most users get direct answers by default.

3 things to know
  1. 1,222 people took part in three randomized experiments
  2. After ChatGPT was removed, accuracy fell and skipping rose
  3. Authors want AI tools to act like tutors by default
Sources: Berkeley News, arXiv paper, AI Weekly
✅ 1,222 participants across three randomized experiments (354, 667, 201)
✅ AI removed after problem 12 of 15; accuracy dropped, skipping rose
✅ Presented at COLM 2026; Brian Christian, co-authors from CMU, MIT, Oxford, UCLA
⚠️ Authors recommend AI default to tutor-like behaviour
