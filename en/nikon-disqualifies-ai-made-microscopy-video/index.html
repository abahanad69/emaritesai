Nikon disqualifies winning microscope video over generative AI

Scientists said the cilia in the first-place video did not look real; Nikon says it broke the contest's AI rules.

2026-10-10 · EmaritesAI

Nikon has stripped the top prize from the winner of its Small World in Motion competition, which accepts video taken through microscopes, after deciding the entry broke its rules on generative AI.

The winning video, submitted by Dr Ning Xu of Tsinghua University, was presented as showing cilia, tiny hair-like structures, moving in the airway of a child with primary ciliary dyskinesia, a respiratory condition known as PCD. After it won, several scientists, including former competition judges, told Nikon on LinkedIn that it did not look real.

Dr Robert Hirst of the University of Leicester, who leads the scientific work at the NHS centre for PCD diagnosis there, told the BBC the cells and cilia looked nothing like those of PCD patients. Xu wrote on LinkedIn that an unsupervised neural network was used for AI-assisted post-processing of images from super-resolution optical imaging. He told reporters he believed he had acted within the rules and cooperated with the review.

Nikon said it reached its decision after re-evaluating the video and supporting materials and consulting its judges. It said the ruling is not a judgement of the entrant's reputation, scientific work or intent. A video by Nguyen Nam Nhat now holds first place, and Nikon says it will review its rules and evaluation process for future entries in the contest, which has run for 15 years.
