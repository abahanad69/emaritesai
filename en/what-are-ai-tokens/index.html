What are tokens, and why does AI pricing count them?

Models read and write text in small pieces, and those pieces set both the bill and the limits.

2026-10-11 · EmaritesAI

AI models do not read whole words the way you do. A tokenizer first cuts the text into tokens, which can be a full word, part of a word, a space or a punctuation mark. The model reads those tokens and writes its answer one token at a time.

OpenAI gives a rule of thumb for English: one token is about four characters, or roughly three quarters of a word, so 100 tokens is about 75 words. It warns that other languages have different ratios, and that the same text can produce different counts on different models.

Tokens matter because they are the unit of billing. API providers usually charge per million tokens, and input, cached input and output can each have their own price. This week Microsoft priced its new Decision-1 model at $0.042 per million input tokens with free output, an unusual split. OpenAI notes that reasoning models also bill their hidden thinking as output tokens.

Tokens also set the limits. A model's context window, the amount of text it can consider in one request, is measured in tokens, and there is a separate cap on how much it can write back. A lower price per token does not always mean a cheaper job, OpenAI says, so test with your real tasks.

Explained simply
  1. Text becomes pieces: A tokenizer splits text into tokens: whole words, parts of words, spaces or punctuation.
  2. English rule of thumb: OpenAI says one token is about 4 characters, so 100 tokens is roughly 75 words.
  3. Prices are per token: APIs charge per million tokens, often with separate input and output prices.
  4. Limits use tokens too: The context window and the maximum reply length are both counted in tokens.
3 things to know
  1. Tokens are the small text pieces models read and write
  2. In English, 100 tokens is roughly 75 words, per OpenAI
  3. Prices, context windows and reply limits are counted in tokens
